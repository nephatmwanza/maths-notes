1.2 Sums of Independent Random Variables

Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be independent random variables and \(Z=X+Y\), where \(X\) and \(Y\) are continuous random variables. \begin {align*} F_Z(z) &=P(Z\leq z)=P(X+Y\leq z)\\ & =\iint \limits _{x+y\leq z}f_{X,Y}(x,y)\,dx\,dy\\ & =\iint \limits _{x+y\leq z}f_X(x)f_Y(y)\,dx\,dy\\ &=\int ^{\infty }_{-\infty }\int ^{z-y}_{-\infty }f_X(x)\,f_Y(y)\,dx\,dy \end {align*}

Hence \[F_{X+Y}(z)=\int ^{\infty }_{-\infty }F_X(z-y)f_Y(y)dy.\] Differentiating with respect to \(z\), we obtain \[f_Z(z)=\int ^{\infty }_{-\infty }f_X(z-y)\,f_Y(y)\,dy.\]

Example 1.2.1. Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be independent Uniformly distributed random variables on \((0,2)\), find the pdf of \(X+Y\) \[f_X(x)=f_Y(y)=\frac {1}{2},\hspace {0.5cm} x,y\in (0,2).\] If \(0 < z < 2\) then \[f_Z(z)=\int ^z_0\frac {1}{2}\,\cdot \,\frac {1}{2}\,dy=\frac {z}{4}\] if, \(2 < z < 4\) we get \[f_Z(z)=\int _{z-2}^2\frac {1}{4}\,dy=\frac {y}{4}\Big |^2_{z-2}=1-\frac {z}{4}.\] Therefore, \[ f_Z(z)= \begin {cases} \frac {z}{4},& 0<z<2\\ 1-\frac {z}{4},&2<z<4\\ \end {cases} \]

Distribution of the Sum of two Independent Poisson Distributions

Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be independent Poisson random variables with mean \(\mu _1\) and \(\mu _2\) respectively. Find the distribution of \(Z=X+Y\) \begin {align*} P(Z=z) &=P(X+Y=z) =\sum ^z_{x=0}P(X=x, Y=z-x)\\ &=\sum ^z_{x=0}P(X=x)P(Y=z-x)\\ &=\sum ^z_{x=0}\frac {\mu _1^xe^{-\mu _1}}{x!}\,\frac {\mu _2^{z-x}e^{-\mu }}{(z-x)!}\\ &=e^{-(\mu _1+\mu _2)}\sum ^z_{x=0}\frac {\mu _1^x\mu _2^{z-x}}{x!(z-x)!}\\ &=\frac {e^{-(\mu _1+\mu _2)}}{z!}\sum ^z_{x=0}\binom {z}{x} \mu _1^x\mu ^{z-x}_2\\ &=\frac {e^{-(\mu _1+\mu _2)}}{z!}(\mu _1+\mu _2)^z\\\\ &=\frac {(\mu _1+\mu _2)^ze^{-(\mu _1+\mu _2)}}{z!}. \end {align*}

Therefore, \[P(Z=z)=\frac {(\mu _1+\mu _2)^ze^{-(\mu _1+\mu _2)}}{z!},\hspace {0.5cm} Z\thicksim P(\mu _1+\mu _2)\]

Note.

1.
If \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent, then \[M_Z(t)=E\left (e^{tZ}\right )=E\left (e^{(Xt+Yt)}\right )=E\left (e^{Xt}\right )E\left (e^{Yt}\right )=M_X(t)M_Y(t).\]
2.
If \(X_1,X_2,\dots ,X_n\) are independent Poisson such that \(E(X_j)=\mu _j\). Then \[Z\thicksim \sum \limits ^n_{j=1}\thicksim P\left (\sum \limits ^n_{j=1}\right ).\]

Prove by mathematical induction.
