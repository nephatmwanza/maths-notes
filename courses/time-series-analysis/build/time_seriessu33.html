13.1 Estimating the mean

The natural estimate of the mean \(\mu \) of a stationary process is the sample mean \(\bar x\). It is unbiased, but its variance is not the familiar \(\sigma ^2/n\), because the observations are correlated.

Theorem 13.1.1. For a stationary process with variance \(\sigma ^2\) and ac.f. \(\rho (k)\), \[ \Var (\bar X) = \frac {\sigma ^2}{n}\Bigl [1 + 2\sum _{k=1}^{n-1}\Bigl (1 - \frac kn\Bigr )\rho (k)\Bigr ] . \] For an AR(1) process with parameter \(\alpha \) and large \(n\), this is approximately \(\dfrac {\sigma ^2}{n}\cdot \dfrac {1 + \alpha }{1 - \alpha }\).

Proof. \(\Var (\bar X) = \frac 1{n^2}\sum _{s=1}^n\sum _{t=1}^n\Cov (X_s, X_t) = \frac {\sigma ^2}{n^2}\sum _s\sum _t\rho (t - s)\). The \(n\) diagonal terms give \(n\), and for each lag \(k \ge 1\) there are \(n - k\) pairs with \(t - s = k\) and another \(n - k\) with \(s - t = k\), so the double sum is \(n + 2\sum _{k=1}^{n-1}(n - k)\rho (k)\); dividing by \(n^2\) gives the formula. For an AR(1), \(\rho (k) = \alpha ^k\), and for large \(n\) the factors \(1 - k/n\) are close to \(1\) wherever \(\alpha ^k\) is not negligible, so the bracket is close to \(1 + 2\sum _{k\ge 1}\alpha ^k = 1 + 2\alpha /(1 - \alpha ) = (1 + \alpha )/(1 - \alpha )\). □

With positive autocorrelation, the sample mean is much less precise than it would be with independent observations. For an AR(1) with \(\alpha = 0.6\) the factor is \(1.6/0.4 = 4\) (with \(n = 100\) the exact bracket is \(3.92\)): \(100\) correlated observations give the precision of only about \(25\) independent ones, an effective sample size of \(25\). A confidence interval for \(\mu \) computed as if the data were independent would be half as wide as it should be.
