2.3 Transformations

A plot may suggest transforming the series, for example by taking logarithms or square roots. There are three main reasons:

1.
to stabilise the variance;
2.
to make the seasonal effect additive;
3.
to make the data closer to normally distributed.

The logarithm does the first two jobs at once when the series is multiplicative, as the next result shows.

Proposition 2.3.1.

(i)
If \(x_t = m_t\,s_t\,\varepsilon _t\) with all factors positive, then \(\log x_t = \log m_t + \log s_t + \log \varepsilon _t\), an additive model.
(ii)
If the standard deviation of \(x_t\) is proportional to its mean \(\mu _t\), say \(\operatorname {sd}(x_t) = c\,\mu _t\), then approximately \(\Var (\log x_t) \approx c^2\), which does not depend on \(t\).

Proof. (i) is the rule \(\log (abc) = \log a + \log b + \log c\). For (ii), by Taylor’s theorem \(\log x_t \approx \log \mu _t + (x_t - \mu _t)/\mu _t\) when \(x_t\) is close to \(\mu _t\). The right-hand side has variance \(\Var (x_t)/\mu _t^2 = c^2\mu _t^2/\mu _t^2 = c^2\). □

So when the seasonal swings, or the scatter, of a series grow with its level, take logarithms first.
