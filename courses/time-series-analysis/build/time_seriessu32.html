11.3 Practice problems

Problem 11.3.1. A stationary process has \(\rho (1) = 0.5\), \(\rho (2) = 0.3\) and \(\rho (3) = 0.1\). Find \(\phi _{11}\), \(\phi _{22}\) and \(\phi _{33}\).

Show solution

Solution. \(\phi _{11} = 0.5\) and \(\phi _{22} = (0.3 - 0.25)/(1 - 0.25) = 0.067\). For \(\phi _{33}\), solve \[ \begin {pmatrix} 1 & 0.5 & 0.3 \\ 0.5 & 1 & 0.5 \\ 0.3 & 0.5 & 1 \end {pmatrix}\begin {pmatrix} \phi _{31} \\ \phi _{32} \\ \phi _{33} \end {pmatrix} = \begin {pmatrix} 0.5 \\ 0.3 \\ 0.1 \end {pmatrix} , \] which gives \(\phi _{31} = 0.473\), \(\phi _{32} = 0.113\), \(\phi _{33} = -0.098\). (Check the first equation: \(0.473 + 0.5(0.113) + 0.3(-0.098) = 0.500\).)

Problem 11.3.2. For the MA(1) process \(X_t = Z_t - 0.6Z_{t-1}\), find \(\phi _{11}\) and \(\phi _{22}\).

Show solution

Solution. \(\rho (1) = -0.6/1.36 = -0.441\), so \(\phi _{11} = -0.441\) and, by Theorem 11.1.4(ii), \(\phi _{22} = -0.1946/(1 - 0.1946) = -0.242\). Both are negative, and the partial ac.f. continues to decay with the same sign, while the ac.f. is zero from lag \(2\).

Problem 11.3.3. Show directly from the definition that, for an AR(1) process, \(\phi _{33} = 0\).

Show solution

Solution. By Theorem 11.1.4(i) with \(p = 1\), the system for \(k = 3\) is solved by \((\phi _{31}, \phi _{32}, \phi _{33}) = (\alpha , 0, 0)\). Directly: with \(\rho (j) = \alpha ^j\), \(\sum _i\phi _{3i}\rho (j - i) = \alpha \rho (j - 1) = \alpha ^j = \rho (j)\) for \(j = 1, 2, 3\), so the equations hold and \(\phi _{33} = 0\).

Problem 11.3.4. From \(n = 100\) observations, two series gave:

Series A
Series B
\(k\) 1 2 3 4 1 2 3 4
\(r_k\) 0.62 0.05 \(-0.08\) 0.04 0.75 0.56 0.41 0.33
\(\hat \phi _{kk}\) 0.62 \(-0.55\) 0.31 \(-0.24\) 0.75 0.01 \(-0.04\) 0.09

Suggest a model for each.

Show solution

Solution. The band is \(\pm 0.2\). Series A: the ac.f. cuts off after lag \(1\) and the partial ac.f. tails off, alternating: MA(1). Since \(r_1 = 0.62\) exceeds \(\frac 12\), the largest possible for an MA(1) (Theorem 6.2.1), the fit should be checked carefully; sampling error can push \(r_1\) over the bound. Series B: the partial ac.f. cuts off after lag \(1\) and the ac.f. decays roughly geometrically (\(0.75^2 = 0.56\), \(0.75^3 = 0.42\)): AR(1) with \(\hat \alpha \approx 0.75\).
