2.6 Practice problems

Problem 2.6.1. Compute the simple moving average of order \(3\) for the series \(5, 8, 6, 9, 7, 10, 8\), and explain why it cannot be computed at the first and last time points.

Show solution

Solution. The averages at \(t = 2, \dots , 6\) are \((5 + 8 + 6)/3 = 6.33\), \((8 + 6 + 9)/3 = 7.67\), \((6 + 9 + 7)/3 = 7.33\), \((9 + 7 + 10)/3 = 8.67\) and \((7 + 10 + 8)/3 = 8.33\). The series zig-zags, and the moving average smooths it into a gently rising trend. At \(t = 1\) there is no \(x_0\), and at \(t = 7\) no \(x_8\), so the symmetric window does not fit.

Problem 2.6.2. Quarterly ratios \(x_t/\hat m_t\) averaged over several years gave the raw multiplicative seasonal indices \(0.90\), \(1.05\), \(1.20\), \(0.89\). Adjust them to average \(1\), and seasonally adjust a third-quarter value of \(48\).

Show solution

Solution. The raw indices add to \(4.04\), an average of \(1.01\), so divide each by \(1.01\): \(0.891\), \(1.040\), \(1.188\), \(0.881\). The adjusted value of the third-quarter observation is \(48/1.188 = 40.4\): third quarters run about \(19\%\) above trend.

Problem 2.6.3. Show that the \(2\times 12\) centred moving average has weights \(\frac {1}{24}\) on \(x_{t\pm 6}\) and \(\frac {1}{12}\) on \(x_{t-5}, \dots , x_{t+5}\), and that it removes any seasonal pattern of period \(12\) whose effects add to zero.

Show solution

Solution. The two twelve-term averages straddling \(t\) cover \(x_{t-6}, \dots , x_{t+5}\) and \(x_{t-5}, \dots , x_{t+6}\). Averaging them, \(x_{t-5}, \dots , x_{t+5}\) appear in both, with weight \(\frac 12(\frac 1{12} + \frac 1{12}) = \frac 1{12}\), and \(x_{t-6}\), \(x_{t+6}\) in one only, with weight \(\frac 1{24}\). For the seasonal part, \(s_{t+6} = s_{t-6}\), so the weighted sum is \(\frac 1{12}(s_{t-6} + s_{t-5} + \dots + s_{t+5})\), a twelfth of the sum over a whole year, which is \(0\).

Problem 2.6.4. For the series \(x_t = t\), \(t = 1, \dots , 10\), compute \(r_1\) and \(r_5\), and comment.

Show solution

Solution. \(\bar x = 5.5\), and \(u_t = t - 5.5\) runs from \(-4.5\) to \(4.5\) in steps of \(1\), with \(\sum u_t^2 = 82.5\). For lag \(1\), \(\sum _{t=1}^9u_tu_{t+1} = 57.75\), so \(r_1 = 0.70\). For lag \(5\), \(\sum _{t=1}^5u_tu_{t+5} = (-4.5)(0.5) + (-3.5)(1.5) + (-2.5)(2.5) + (-1.5)(3.5) + (-0.5)(4.5) = -21.25\), so \(r_5 = -0.258\). The coefficients of a short trending series fall steadily (here they turn negative because with \(n = 10\) the two ends of the series are paired); for a long trending series they stay positive and large for many lags. Either way the correlogram mainly reflects the trend, which should be removed before the correlogram is interpreted.
