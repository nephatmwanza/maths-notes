10.1 Definition

When a process is non-stationary, the non-stationary sources of variation must be removed. A common approach, which goes back to Box and Jenkins, is to difference the series. If in the ARMA model \[ X_t = \alpha _1X_{t-1} + \dots + \alpha _pX_{t-p} + Z_t + \beta _1Z_{t-1} + \dots + \beta _qZ_{t-q} \] \(X_t\) is replaced by its \(d\)th difference \(\nabla ^dX_t\), the model is capable of describing a certain type of non-stationary series. Writing \[ W_t = \nabla ^dX_t = (1 - B)^dX_t , \] the model is the ARMA process \[ W_t = \alpha _1W_{t-1} + \dots + \alpha _pW_{t-p} + Z_t + \beta _1Z_{t-1} + \dots + \beta _qZ_{t-q} , \] or \(\phi (B)W_t = \theta (B)Z_t\), or, in terms of the original series, \[ \phi (B)(1 - B)^dX_t = \theta (B)Z_t . \] Thus \(W_t\) is a stationary ARMA(\(p, q\)) process, while \(X_t\), described through its \(d\)th difference, is an autoregressive integrated moving average process of order \((p, d, q)\), ARIMA(\(p, d, q\)). The name “integrated” is explained by the case \(d = 1\): then \(X_t = X_0 + W_1 + W_2 + \dots + W_t\), a sum (the discrete analogue of an integral) of the stationary \(W\)’s.

The model for \(X_t\) is non-stationary: its generalised AR operator \(\phi (B)(1 - B)^d\) has \(d\) roots on the unit circle, at \(B = 1\), rather than outside it. In practice \(d\) is usually \(1\), occasionally \(2\). The second difference is \begin {align*} \nabla ^2X_t &= \nabla (X_t - X_{t-1}) \\ &= (X_t - X_{t-1}) - (X_{t-1} - X_{t-2}) \\ &= X_t - 2X_{t-1} + X_{t-2} , \end {align*}

which is \((1 - B)^2X_t = (1 - 2B + B^2)X_t\), as the binomial expansion says. Special cases are familiar: ARIMA(0,1,0) is the random walk of Section 5, and ARIMA(0,0,\(q\)) and ARIMA(\(p\),0,0) are the MA and AR processes.

Example 10.1.1. Write the ARIMA(1,1,0) model \((1 - 0.6B)(1 - B)X_t = Z_t\) as a difference equation in \(X_t\), and find the roots of its generalised AR operator. Given \(x_{n-1} = 50\) and \(x_n = 54\), find the value of \(X_{n+1}\) if the next shock is \(0\).

Solution. \((1 - 0.6B)(1 - B) = 1 - 1.6B + 0.6B^2\), so \[ X_t = 1.6X_{t-1} - 0.6X_{t-2} + Z_t . \] The roots of \(1 - 1.6B + 0.6B^2 = 0\) are \(B = 1\) and \(B = 1/0.6 = 1.67\): one unit root, from the differencing, and one stationary root. As an AR(2) this would fail the triangle condition, since \(\alpha _1 + \alpha _2 = 1.6 - 0.6 = 1\), exactly on the boundary. With \(Z_{n+1} = 0\), \(X_{n+1} = 1.6(54) - 0.6(50) = 56.4\). In terms of differences: the last change was \(W_n = 4\), the next is \(0.6 \times 4 = 2.4\), and \(54 + 2.4 = 56.4\). The changes die away geometrically, but the level never returns to any fixed mean.
