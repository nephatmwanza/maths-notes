19.1 Problems on the whole course

Problem 19.1.1. Show that the MA(1) process \(X_t = Z_t + \beta Z_{t-1}\) has \(\rho (1) = \beta /(1 + \beta ^2)\) and \(\rho (\tau ) = 0\) for \(\tau \ge 2\). Deduce the largest value \(|\rho (1)|\) can take.

Show solution

Solution. With \(\Var (Z_t) = \sigma ^2\), \[ \gamma (0) = (1 + \beta ^2)\sigma ^2 , \qquad \gamma (1) = \beta \sigma ^2 , \qquad \gamma (\tau ) = 0 \ (\tau \ge 2) , \] the last because \(X_t\) and \(X_{t+\tau }\) then share no shock. Dividing, \[ \rho (1) = \frac {\beta }{1 + \beta ^2} , \qquad \rho (\tau ) = 0 \ (\tau \ge 2) . \] Differentiating, \(\rho (1)\) is extremised at \(\beta = \pm 1\), giving \[ |\rho (1)| \le \tfrac 12 . \] So a sample first autocorrelation beyond \(0.5\) in magnitude rules out an MA(1) outright, whatever \(\beta \) is chosen: a genuine identification constraint, not merely a guideline (Theorem 6.2.1).

Note also that \(\beta \) and \(1/\beta \) give the same \(\rho (1)\): the autocorrelations do not determine the model, which is why invertibility is imposed, choosing \(|\beta | < 1\), so that the root of \(\theta (B)\) lies outside the unit circle.

Problem 19.1.2. An AR(2) process is \(X_t = 0.5X_{t-1} + 0.3X_{t-2} + Z_t\). Determine whether it is stationary, and find \(\rho (1)\) and \(\rho (2)\).

Show solution

Solution. Stationarity requires the roots of \(1 - 0.5z - 0.3z^2 = 0\) to lie outside the unit circle. Solving, \[ z = \frac {0.5 \pm \sqrt {0.25 + 1.2}}{-0.6} = \frac {0.5 \pm 1.204}{-0.6} , \] giving \(z \approx -2.84\) and \(z \approx 1.17\). Both exceed one in modulus, so the process is stationary. (Equivalently, \(\alpha _1 + \alpha _2 = 0.8 < 1\), \(\alpha _2 - \alpha _1 = -0.2 < 1\) and \(\alpha _2 > -1\): the point is inside the triangle of Theorem 8.3.1.)

The Yule–Walker equations give \begin {align*} \rho (1) &= \frac {\alpha _1}{1 - \alpha _2} = \frac {0.5}{0.7} = 0.714 , \\ \rho (2) &= \alpha _1\rho (1) + \alpha _2 = 0.5(0.714) + 0.3 = 0.657 . \end {align*}

The slow decay is characteristic of an AR process: the ac.f. tails off rather than cutting off, and it is the partial ac.f. that would cut off after lag two.

Problem 19.1.3. A series shows a clear upward trend and yearly seasonality in monthly data. Describe the differencing needed before an ARMA model is fitted, and say what model class results.

Show solution

Solution. A trend is removed by first differencing, \(\nabla X_t = X_t - X_{t-1}\). Yearly seasonality in monthly data is removed by seasonal differencing at lag twelve, \(\nabla _{12}X_t = X_t - X_{t-12}\). Applying both, \[ \nabla \nabla _{12}X_t = (X_t - X_{t-1}) - (X_{t-12} - X_{t-13}) . \] Fitting an ARMA model to the result gives a SARIMA \((p, 1, q) \times (P, 1, Q)_{12}\) model (Section 15).

Two cautions. Differencing more than necessary inflates the variance and introduces artificial negative autocorrelation (Theorem 10.2.1), so one difference of each kind is usually enough and the ac.f. should be inspected before applying a second. And a deterministic trend is better removed by regression than by differencing: the two are different models, and differencing a deterministic trend leaves a non-invertible moving-average term behind.

Problem 19.1.4. After fitting an ARMA model, the residual autocorrelations look like white noise but the autocorrelations of the squared residuals decay slowly. What does this indicate and what should be done?

Show solution

Solution. Uncorrelated residuals mean the model has captured the conditional mean correctly, so the ARMA orders need no revision. Correlated squared residuals mean the conditional variance is not constant (large errors follow large errors), which violates the constant-variance assumption of the ARMA model.

The remedy is to add a variance model: fit an ARCH or GARCH term to the residuals, so the mean follows the ARMA structure and the variance follows the volatility structure (Section 18). Ignoring it does not bias the point forecasts, but every interval built from the model will be wrong: too wide in quiet periods and far too narrow in turbulent ones, which is precisely when the interval matters.
