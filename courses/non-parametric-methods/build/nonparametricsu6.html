1.6 Non- Parametric Test

-
Some statistics for NP tests have at least approximately familiar continuous distributions such as the Normal,\(t\), \(F\) or \(\chi ^2\) distributions.
-
We shall also use some statistics with discontinuous distributions.

Example
We want to test the \(H_0\) that the median \(\theta \) of survival times for the population is \(200\) vs \(H_1\) of some other valise i.e \(H_0:\theta =200\hspace {0.3cm} \text {vs}\hspace {0.3cm} H_1:\theta \neq 200\)

-
test \(H_0\) at \(\alpha =0.05/5\%\) level.
-
Steps to take when carrying out the test: (1) Statement, (2) C.R, (3) Test Statistic, (4) Decision Rule, (5) Conclusion.

-
A simple test need a count of the number of sample values \(>\) 200. Recording each as ”\(+\)” sign.
-
According to definition of R.S and of population median. A r.s from any distribution with 200 each sample value is equally likely to be above or below 200.
-
This implies that under \(H_0\) the number of ”\(+\)” sign in 10 observations when \(P=1/2\); given by \[P(R=r)=C^N_rP^r(1-P)^{N-r}\] \[P_r=P(X=r)=C^{10}_r\Big (\frac {1}{2}\Big )^{10}\hspace {0.4cm} X\thicksim B\Big (10,\frac {1}{2}\Big )\]

-
If \(R=3\) i.e 3 observations exceed 200.
-
\(\implies \) There are \(3\) ’\(+\)’ signs in a sample of \(10\). \[\text {C.R}:\hspace {0.5cm} R\leq 3\hspace {0.5cm}\text {or}\hspace {0.5cm} R\geq 7\]
-
\(\hspace {0.4cm}\displaystyle { P(R\leq 3)=\sum ^3_{r=0}C^{10}_r\Big (\frac {1}{2}\Big )^{10}=0.1719}.\) when \(H_0\) is true.
-
Since the probability of getting 3 or less ”\(+\)” signs \(P(R\leq 3)\) or probability of getting 7 or more ”\(+\)” signs \(P(R\geq 7)\) is \begin {align*} P(R\leq 3)+P(R\geq 7) &=2P(R\leq 3)\hspace {0.3cm} \text {by symmetry}\\ &=0.3438 \end {align*}

Example
The heart rate [beats/m] when standing was recorded for seven members of a tutorial group. We assume that in population of students the distribution of heart rates when standing is symmetric.
Given observations of 73, 82, 87, 68, 106, 60, 97

Test the hypothesis: \(H_0:\theta =70\hspace {0.4cm}\text {vs}\hspace {0.4cm} H_1:\theta >70\)

symmetry implies that under \(H_0\) sample values should have a near symmetry scatter about \(\theta = 70\).

So the magnitude of the sum of \(+ve\) and \(-ve\) deviations from \(70\) (the d\(_i\)) should not differ greatly.

-
The one sided alternative \(H_1\implies \) that when it holds there is likely to be more \(+ve\) deviations from \(70\) than there would be under \(H_0\) and that these \(+ve\) deviations will tend to be larger than the \(-ve\) deviations.
-
For the given data deviations from 70 are \(3, 12, 17, -2, 36, -10,27,X_i-70,\hspace {0.5cm} i=1,2,\dots ,7\)
-
Under \(H_0\) the sum of the \(+ve\) and negative deviations should nearly equal where as under \(H_1\) we expect the sum of positives to be larger than the sum of negative deviations.
-
So the sum of positive deviations (differences from 70) is a reasonable statistic to assess the strength of evidence against \(H_0\) in favor of \(H_1\).
-
If \(H_1\) had been two sided \(H_1:\theta \neq 70\), small as well as large values of positive deviations would also support \(H_1\).
-
For randomized test based on sum of positive deviations \(S_+\), the sum of the positive deviations would also have supported \(H_1\).
-
The key to obtaining the randomised distribution of \(S_+\) when \(H_0\) is true/ holds lies in the fact that under \(H_0\) the sign of each deviation is equally likely, on account of symmetry assumption to be \(+ve\) or negative \(-ve\).
-
Therefore any combination of \(+ve\) and \(-ve\) sign attached to a set of deviations having the observed magnitude is equally likely.
-
In the 7 observations there are \(2^7=128\) possible allocations of signs (2 to each of the 7 deviations) each allocation being equally likely under \(H_0\).
-
If we want to obtain the \(P\)-value when, that is small because we then only need to know how many of the equally likely allocations of signs given an \(S_+\) greater than or equal to that for our sample i.e \(S_+=3+12+17+2+36+10+27=107\)
-
The next highest \(S_+(105)\) is obtained when only then smallest deviation, 2, has a \(-ve\) sign.
-
In order of decreasing magnitude it can be verified that the other sums greater than the observed \(S_+=95\) occur only when \(-ve\) signs are attached to 3 only \((104)\), 2 and 3 \((102)\) and 10 only \((97)\).
-
Attaching \(-ve\) to 12 only or to both 10 and 2 the sum is 95.
-
Therefore there are seven sums greater than or equal to the observed \(S_+=95\).
-
Since all are equally likely, the \(P\)-value for assessing the strength of evidence against \(H_0\hspace {0.3cm}\text {is}\hspace {0.3cm} P=\dfrac {7}{128}\thickapprox 0.055\hspace {0.5cm} P=P-\text {value}\)
-
The test is called Pitmum test.
