1.3 Hypothesis Tests

-
Estimation is a key aim of statistical analysis.
-
Often t-test is often used to test hypothesis about the unknown mean \(\mu \) of a ND we specify a null hypothesis to the \(\mu \) takes a specific value \(\mu _0\) and alternative hypothesis that it takes some other value \(H_0:\mu =\mu _0\hspace {0.3cm}\text {vs}\hspace {0.3cm} H_1:\mu \neq \mu _0\)
-
test statistic \(t\) is a function of sample values computed by a formula.
-
The procedure requires that Tables are used to compare the magnitude of the calculated \(t=|t|\) with \(t_{\alpha }\) given by the Tables.
i.e \(t_{\alpha }\) chosen such that when \(H_0\) was true \(P_r(|t|\geq t_{\alpha })=\alpha .\)
-
In practice \(\alpha \) is usually \(0.05\hspace {0.1cm}(5\%), \hspace {0.3cm}0.01\hspace {0.1cm}(1\%),\hspace {0.3cm}0.001\hspace {0.1cm}(1\%)\) known as significance levels.
-
If we obtain a value \(|t|\geq t_{\alpha }\) the results are are said to be significant at probability level \(\alpha \) or at corresponding \(100\alpha \) percent level.
-
These are often referred to as significantly \((5\%)\) highly significant \((1\%)\) and very high significance \((0.01\%)\).
-
If significance is attained, we speak of rejecting the \(H_0\) at the \(\alpha \) level.
-
If significance is not attained, the \(H_0\) is said to be that accepted at level \(\alpha \).
-
Non significance \(\implies \) \(H_0\) is true while significance \(\implies \) \(H_0\) is false.
