1.1 Non-Parametric Methods

-
Sometimes parametric inference is inappropriate or even impossible.
-
To assume that a sample comes from any specified family of distributions may be unreasonable e.g exam results on a percentage scale for each candidate is bounded and ordered grades designated Grade A, B, C e.t.c. Given these number for two different schools we may not know if they indicate a difference in performances between schools that might be due to unequal standards of teaching or ability of one school to attract more able pupils. The methods of inference is usually non-parametric.
-
Even with precise measurements it may be irrational to assume ND because normality implies certain properties of symmetry and spread.
-
We may be able to see a simple is not from a ND simply by looking at sample measurement of central tendency like mean and median and measure of variation like standard deviation and range.
-
There are well known distributions that are flatter than normal or skew. Therefore we are often able to say little more than that our sample appears to come from a distribution that is skew or very pecked of very flat e.t.c.
-
NP inference may be appropriate.
-
NP methods are referred to as distribution free.
-
The term relate not to be the distribution of the test statistics but to the fact that the method can be applied to samples from population having distributions which need only be specified in broad ed terms e.g as being continuous, symmetric, identical, differing only in mean and median e.t.c.
-
NP methods often have weaker assumption about distribution than the parametric methods.
-
If a parametric test does not depend critically on assumption that samples come from a distribution in a particular family, then a NP test needing weaker assumption may be preferred.
-
NP methods are in no sense preferred methods of analysis for all situations. A strength in their applicability where there is insufficient theory or data to justify or test compatibility with specific distribution model.
-
Tests and estimation procedure for measurement data often about average or centrality measure mean and medians are widely used to indicate where distributions are centred.
-
They are also known as measures of location/centrality.
-
Measures of dispersion/spread often measured by variance of SD are also widely used.
