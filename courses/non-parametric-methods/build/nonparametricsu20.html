3.11 Wilcoxon Rank Sum

The ranks of \(X's\) in the combined ordered arrangement of the two sample would generally be larger than the ranks of \(Y's\) if the median of the \(X\) population exceeds the median of the \(Y\) population. Wilcoxon \((1945)\) proposed a test were accept \(H_1:\theta <0\) \(X>Y\). If the sum of the ranks of \(X'\) is too small and 2-sided location alternative \(H_1:\theta \neq 0\) if the sum of the ranks of the \(X's\)is too large or too small.
The function of the ranks expressed as a linear rank statistic has the sample weights
\(G_i=i,\hspace {0.3cm} i=1,2,\dots ,N\).

The Wilcoxon Rank-Sum statistic is given by \(\displaystyle {W_N=\sum ^N_{i=1}iZ_i\hspace {0.3cm} \text {where}}\) \[ Z_i= \begin {cases} 1 &\text {ith r.v in combined ordered sample is x }\\ 0 & \text {otherwise} \end {cases} \] for \(i=1,2,\dots ,N\) with \(N=n+m\)

Note:

-
If the Table does not cater for sample size give use of the normal approximation.
-
Smaller sample size \((n)\) is on the vertical and large sample size \((m)\) is on the horizontal line in the Table of critical values.
-
\(W\) is small sample test statistic.

Example
A researcher is interested is seeing if men download more movies than women. The random sample recorded 4 men and 4 women and recorded how many GB of movies they had.

-
\(H_0\): Men keep more movies on computer than Women.
-
\(n=4\) and \(m=4\).
-
We want an upper tail critical value since we want to know if Men have more movies on the computers at \(\alpha =0.05\).
-
From the table, we would reject \(H_0\) if observed \(W>11\).
-
To find \(W\), combine the data and rank them.
-
Summing ranks relating to Men, \(W\) have \(W=20\).
-
Since \(W=20>W_{C.V}=11\), we reject \(H_0\).

Note:
When computing \(W\):

-
Calculate the sum of the ranks for the groups with lower/ smaller \(n\).
-
If groups are equal \((n)\) calculate the sum of the ranks for each group and take the smaller rank sum.

Example:
Let’s suppose we have the following 2 random samples with \(n_1=10\) and \(n_2=11\).

-
\(H_0\): \(D_1\) and \(D_2\) are identical
\(H_1:\) \(D_1\) is shifted left of \(D_2\).
-
Test Statistic \begin {align*} Z &=\frac {W-\dfrac {n_1(n_1+n_2+1)}{2}}{\sqrt {\dfrac {n_1n_2(n_1+n_2+1)}{12}}}\\\\ E(W) &=\frac {n_1(n_1+n_2+1)}{2}\\\\ \text {var}(W) &=\frac {n_1n_2(n_1+n_2+1)}{12}\\\\ Z &=\frac {57-\dfrac {10(10+11+1)}{2}}{\sqrt {\dfrac {10\times 11(10+11+1)}{12}}}\\\\ &=-3.73 \end {align*}
-
Rejection when \((\alpha =0.05)\) table value \(Z_{0.05}=-1.645\)
-
Reject \(H_0\)
