3.4 Quartile Functions

-
A quartile of a function Cdf \(F_X\) of a random variable \(X\) is a real number that divides the area under the pdf into two parts of specified amounts.
-
Only area to the left need to be specified.
-
The P\(^{th}\) quartile (100 P\(^{th}\) percentile) of \(F_X\) is that value of \(X_1\), \(X_P\) such that 100P percentile of the value of \(X\) is the population, are less than or equal to \(X_P\) for any \(+ve\) \(P(0<P<1)\).
-
\(X_P\) is parameter of the population that satisfies \(P(X\leq X_P)=P\) or \(F_X(X_P)=P\).
-
For strictly increasing cdf of \(X\) the P\(^{th}\) quartile is the unique solution to the equation \[X_P=F_X^{-1}(P)=Q_X(P).\]
-
We call \(Q_X(P)\), \(0<P<1\), then inverse of the cdf, the quartile function of the random variable \(X\).
