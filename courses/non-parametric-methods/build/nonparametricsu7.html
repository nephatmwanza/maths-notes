2.1 Sign Test

-
A sign test is a statistical method for consistent differences between pairs of observations. It is useful in situations where the conditions for the parametric \(z\) or t-test are not true.
-
It is used to test \(H_0\) that the median distribution is equal to some value. It is also used in
*
place of a one sample t-test.
*
place of a paired t-test.
*
for ordered categorical data where a numerical scale is inappropriate but where it is possible to rank observations.

Procedure

-
The observation in a sample of size \(n\) are \(x_1, x_2,\dots ,x_n\).
-
These could be paired differences from correlated/ matched observations.
-
\(H_0\) is that the population median is equal to some value \(M\).
-
Suppose that \(N_+\) (number of ”\(+\)” signs) of these observations are greater than \(M\) and \(N_-\) (number of ”\(-\)” signs) are smaller than \(M\).
-
In the case of paired test \(M\) would be zero under \(H_0\).
-
Values of \(X\) which are exactly equal to will give a difference \(0\).
-
Values of \(X\) which are equal to \(M\) are ignored.
-
The sum \(N_+ +N_-\) may therefore be less than \(n\), denoted by \(n'\).

Test Procedure

-
Chose an appropriate statistics \(N_+\)/\(N_-\) according to the information provided by \(H\).
-
Use the Binomial distribution to find the probability of observing a value \(N_+\)/\(N_-\) or higher assuming \(P=0.5\) and \(n=n'\).
-
For 1-sided \(H_1\), thus the \(P\)-value.
-
For 2-sided \(H_1\), double the probability obtained to obtain the \(P\)-value.

-
For \(n\geq 25\), the normal distribution approximation can be used. Calculate the \(z\)-value as follows: \[z=\frac {n-2R-1}{\sqrt {n}}\] where \(R\) is \(N_+\) or \(N_-\).
-
And use standard normal tables to find the \(P\)-value.

Tied Scores

-
If a subject has the same score in each condition, there will be no sign, because the difference score is zero.
-
Two methods of handling zero difference are proposed.
(a)
Scores are dropped from the sample and reduced \(n\) by the appropriate number.
(b)
It may not be the best way as can lead to loss of too much power.
-
Best practice is that
*
if one observation/score/subject with tied score drop that subject.
*
if two subjects with tied score, make one a ”\(+\)” and one a ”\(-\)”.
*
generally for odd number of subject \((>1)\) drop one randomly selected subject and then proceed as for even numbered tied scores.

Example 1
A random sample of 10 students weight (Kg) from student population is given as \[135\,,\, 119\,,\, 106\,,\, 135\,,\, 180\, ,\,108\,,\, 128\,,\, 160\,,\, 143\,,\, 175\] Find out as as whether the median weight of students population is different from 140 Kg. Using the sign test \[H_0:\theta =140\hspace {0.5cm} \text {vs}\hspace {0.5cm} H_1:\theta \neq 140\]

\[-\hspace {0.3cm}-\hspace {0.3cm}-\hspace {0.3cm}-\hspace {0.3cm}+\hspace {0.3cm}-\hspace {0.3cm}-\hspace {0.3cm}+\hspace {0.3cm}+\hspace {0.3cm}+\] \[N_+=4,\hspace {1cm}N_+\thicksim B\Big (10,\frac {1}{2}\Big )\]

\[\text {C.R}\hspace {0.5cm} 4\hspace {0.4cm}\text {and}\hspace {0.4cm} 6\] \[P(N_+\leq 4)\cup P(N_+\geq 6)\]

\begin {align*} P-\text {value} &=P(N_+\leq 4)+P(N_+\geq 6)\\ &=2P(N_+\leq 4)\hspace {1cm}\text {by symmetry}\\\\ &=2\sum ^4_{i=0}C_i^{10}\Big (\frac {1}{2}\Big )^{10}\\ &=2(0.376953125)\\ &=0.754\\ \end {align*}

Example 2
A psychologist want to know if monkeys prefer stimulation of brain area A to train area B. In the experiment 14 monkeys are taught to press two bars. When a light comes on, presses for 1 always result in simulation of area A; presses on bars 2 always result in simulation of area B.
After learning to pass the bars, the monkeys are tested for 15 months, during which time the frequencies for two bars are recorded. The data is shown.

Find out whether there is a difference in the two conditions at \(5\%\) level of significance.

Sign test.

Formulation and assumptions
The researcher did not predict a particular outcome but wanted to know if the two conditions differ. There it is non directional i.e

\(H_0\): No differences between frequencies.
\(H_1\): frequencies differ , 2-sided.

\(\therefore \) Extreme number of ‘\(+\)’ signs, smaller or large will support \(H_1\). A middling number of ‘\(+\)’ signs would be consistent with \(H_0\).

Procedure

-
Sampling distribution of the test statistic is the Binomial distribution with \(N=14\) and \(P=0.5\) i.e \(\Big (14,\dfrac {1}{2}\Big )\).
-
To carry out the sign test consider \[S_+=3\hspace {0.5cm}\text {and}\hspace {0.5cm} S_-=11\]

\[N=\min (S_+,S_-)=3\] \[N=3\hspace {1cm}\text {is}\hspace {0.3cm}+\hspace {0.3cm}\text {Signs}\]

-
Since \(H_1\) is non directional/ 2-sided we must find \[P(N\leq 3)+P(N\geq 11)\] \[\text {C.V}:\hspace {0.5cm} 3\hspace {1cm} \text {and}\hspace {1cm} 11\]

\[\text {C.R}\hspace {0.5cm} N\leq 3\hspace {0.5cm}\text {and}\hspace {0.5cm}N\geq 11\] \begin {align*} P-\text {value} &=P(N\leq 3)+P(N\geq 11)\\ &=0.029+(1-P(N<11))\\ &=1.029-0.971\\ &=0.0578 \end {align*}

Conclusion:
No sufficient evidence to allow the rejection of \(H_0\).

17 teachers attend a summer school to improve their listening skills. They were given a pretest and potest: 16 teachers improved, 1 did more poorly.
Did participation improve their performance on listening test? \(\theta =0.05\)
