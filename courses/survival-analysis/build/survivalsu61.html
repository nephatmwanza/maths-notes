11.4 Recurrent events

When the event can recur, each subject contributes a sequence of times, and the data are kept in the counting-process form of the section on checking proportional hazards, one record \((t_{\text {start}}, t_{\text {stop}}]\) per interval between events. A simple descriptive summary is the mean cumulative function \(M(t)\), the expected number of events per subject by time \(t\), estimated by the Nelson–Aalen sum \(\hat M(t) = \sum _{t_j \le t}d_j/n_j\), where \(n_j\) now counts subjects under observation at \(t_j\) whatever their number of previous events. Three regression models are common.

  • The Andersen–Gill model treats each subject’s events as a counting process with intensity \(h_0(t)e^{\beta ^{\mathsf T}x}\), the same for every event. The subject stays in the risk set after each event. Robust standard errors clustered on the subject allow for the dependence between a subject’s events.
  • The Prentice–Williams–Peterson (PWP) model stratifies by event number, so the \(m\)th event has its own baseline hazard and only subjects who have had \(m - 1\) events are at risk of it; it suits events that change the subject, where a second infection is not like the first.
  • The Wei–Lin–Weissfeld marginal model fits a separate Cox model for the time from the start to each event number, with robust standard errors.

Example 11.4.1. In a placebo-controlled trial of interferon gamma for chronic granulomatous disease, an inherited immune disorder, \(128\) patients had \(76\) serious infections: \(56\) among \(65\) on placebo and \(20\) among \(63\) on interferon. The mean cumulative number of infections after a year is \(1.26\) per patient on placebo and \(0.42\) on interferon (Figure 11). The Andersen–Gill model gives a rate ratio of \(0.334\) (\(p = 0.0004\) with the robust standard error \(0.312\), larger than the model-based \(0.261\) because a patient’s infections are correlated); the PWP model gives \(0.423\) (\(p = 0.003\)), and an analysis of the first infection alone gives \(0.335\) (\(p = 0.001\)) (Software 11.5.4). Interferon cuts the rate of infection by about two-thirds.
