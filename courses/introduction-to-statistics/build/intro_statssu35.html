5.4 Linear Regression

When \(X\) and \(Y\) are linearly related, we can draw a scatter diagram. We can show this relationship between \(X\) and \(Y\). Our interest is draw a straight line which approximates the points.

\[Y_i=\alpha +\beta X_i+e_i,\qquad \text {and}\qquad e_i=Y_i-\alpha -\beta X_i\] where \(\alpha +\beta X_i\) represents the linear relationship between \(Y\) and \(X_i\) and \(e_i\) are a random errors. \begin {align*} \beta &=\ \text {coefficient of regression of}\ Y\ \text {and}\ X\\ X&=\ \text {independent variable}\\ Y&=\ \text {dependent variable}\\ \alpha &=\ \text {constant} \end {align*}

We assume that the errors \(e_i\) are independent of one another and of the \(X_i\), and normally distributed with mean 0 and standard deviation \(\sigma _{Y/X}\).
Our interest is to draw this line by estimating \(\alpha \) and \(\beta \).

5.4.1 The Method of Least Squares

The parameters \(\alpha \) and \(\beta \) are estimated by the method of least squares so called because estimates \(\hat {a}\) and \(\hat {b}\) of \(\alpha \) and \(\beta \) respectively are chosen so as to minimise \(\sum \limits _{i=1}^{n}e_i^2\), the sum of the squared errors (\(n\) is the number of points on the scatter diagram). \begin {align*} Y_i &=\alpha +\beta X_i+e_i\\e_i&=Y_i-\alpha -\beta X_i\\ \sum ^n_{i=1} e^2_i &=\sum ^n_{i=1}(Y_i-\alpha -\beta X_i)^2 \end {align*}

This expression can be varied by varying \(\alpha \) and \(\beta \), \(\hat {a}\) and \(\hat {b}\) estimates of \(\alpha \) and \(\beta \). We choose these \(\alpha \) and \(\beta \) so that \(\sum e^2\) are minimised.
The appropriate values of \(\hat {a}\) and \(\hat {b}\) are found by setting the partial derivatives equal to zero, the condition for a stationary point. \[\sum ^n_ie_i^2=\sum ^n_i(Y-\alpha -\beta X)^2\ \text {estimate}\ \alpha \ \text {and}\ \beta \ \text {to become}\ \sum ^n_ie^2_i=\sum ^n_i(Y-\hat {a}-\hat {b}X)^2\] We now take partial derivatives with respect to \(\hat {a}\) and \(\hat {b}\).

\begin {align*} \frac {\partial }{\partial \hat {a}}\left (\sum ^n_i(Y-\hat {a}-\hat {b}X)^2\right )&=0\\[1ex] 2\left (\sum ^n_i(Y-\hat {a}-\hat {b}X)\right )(-1) &=0\\[1ex] -2\sum ^n_i(Y-\hat {a}-\hat {b}X)&=0 \tag {1}\label {eq:ls1} \end {align*}

\begin {align*} \frac {\partial }{\partial \hat {b}}\left (\sum ^n_i(Y-\hat {a}-\hat {b}X)^2\right )&=0\\[1ex] -2\sum ^n_iX(Y-\hat {a}-\hat {b}X)&=0 \tag {2}\label {eq:ls2} \end {align*}

\begin {align*} \text {Now using equation (1) we have the following}\\ \sum ^n_i(Y-\hat {a}-\hat {b}X)&=0\\[1ex] \sum ^n_iY-n\hat {a}-\hat {b}\sum ^n_iX&=0 \tag {3}\label {eq:ls3}\\[1ex] n\hat {a}=\sum ^n_iY-\hat {b}\sum ^n_iX\quad &\implies \quad \hat {a}=\frac {1}{n}\left [\sum ^n_iY-\hat {b}\sum ^n_iX\right ]\\[1ex]\\ \implies \quad \hat {a}&=\overline {Y}-\hat {b}\overline {X} \end {align*}

\begin {align*} \text {Also from } \eqref {eq:ls2} \text {, it follows that}\\ \sum ^n_1X(Y-\hat {a}-\hat {b}X)&=0\\[1ex] \sum ^n_iXY-\hat {a}\sum ^n_iX-\hat {b}\sum ^n_iX^2&=0\\[1ex] \sum ^n_iXY-\sum ^n_iX\left [\frac {1}{n}\left (\sum ^n_iY-\hat {b}\sum ^n_iX\right )\right ]-\hat {b}\sum ^n_iX^2&=0\\[1ex] \sum ^n_iXY-\frac {1}{n}\sum ^n_iX\sum ^n_iY+\frac {1}{n}\hat {b}\left (\sum ^n_iX\right )^2-\hat {b}\sum ^n_iX^2&=0 \end {align*}

\begin {align*} \hat {b}\left [\frac {1}{n}\left (\sum ^n_iX\right )^2-\sum ^n_iX^2\right ]&=\frac {1}{n}\sum ^n_iX\sum ^n_iY-\sum ^n_iXY\\[1ex] \hat {b}&=\frac {\frac {1}{n}\sum \limits ^n_iX\sum \limits ^n_iY-\sum \limits ^n_iXY}{\frac {1}{n}\left (\sum \limits ^n_iX\right )^2-\sum \limits ^n_iX^2}\\[1ex] \hat {b}&=\frac {\sum X\sum Y-n\sum XY}{\left (\sum X\right )^2-n\sum X^2}\\[1ex] \implies \quad \hat {b}=\frac {n\sum XY-\sum X\sum Y}{n\sum X^2-\left (\sum X\right )^2} \end {align*}

Therefore, the relationship between \(X\) and \(Y\) is given by \(Y=\hat {a}+\hat {b}X\,\) and this is called the Regression line of \(Y\) on \(X\).
