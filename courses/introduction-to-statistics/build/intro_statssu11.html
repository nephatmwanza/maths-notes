5.1 Calculation of Correlation Coefficient

The correlation coefficient will be in the interval \([-1,1]\).

5.1.1 Pearson Product-Moment Correlation Coefficient

\begin {align*} \text {(a).}\hspace {1cm} r&=\frac {\sum (X-\overline {X})(Y-\overline {Y})}{\sqrt {\sum (X-\overline {X})^2}\hspace {0.1cm}.\hspace {0.1cm}\sqrt {\sum (Y-\overline {Y})^2}}\\\\\\ \text {(b).}\hspace {1cm} r&=\frac {n\sum XY-\sum X\sum Y}{\sqrt {n\sum X^2-\Bigg (\sum X\Bigg )^2}\sqrt {n\sum Y^2-\Bigg (\sum Y\Bigg )^2}}\\ \end {align*}

Example

5.1.2 Spearmans Rank Correlation Coefficient

In this case we do not have two continuous variables which are normally distributed but two discrete variables in the form of ranking \[r=1-\frac {6\sum d^2}{n(n^2-1)}\] If there is an agreement between the two ranks, then \(r=1\). \(d=\) difference between the two ranks.

Example
Two judges at a competition awarded the following marks to candidates

  1. Calculate the Spearmans rank correlation coefficient.
  2. Do the judges agree on the order in which they place the candidate.

Solution

\begin {align*} r&=1-\frac {6\sum d^2}{n(n^2-1)}=1-\frac {6\times 17.5}{7(7^2-1)}\\\\ \implies \hspace {0.5cm} r&=0.6875 \end {align*}

There is a \(+ve\) fair correlation between the judges. So we have that \(r_s=0.6875\)

Now we solve for the second part.
Set the hypothesis
\(H_0\): The is no correlation between the judges \(r_s=0\)
\(H_1:\) There is an association \(r_s\neq 0\)

\(|r_c|=\) critical value from the table, \(n=7\), \(|r_c|=0.75\)
\[r_s=0.6875\] We Accept \(H_0\) since \(r_s<|r_c|=0.75\).
Therefore, the judges are not in agreement.
