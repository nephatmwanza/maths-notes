1.12 Jacobians

Definition 1.12.1. If \(F(u,v)\) and \(G(u,v)\) are differentiable in a region, the Jacobian of \(F\) and \(G\) with respect to \(u\) and \(v\) is the determinant \[ \frac {\partial (F,G)}{\partial (u,v)} = \begin {vmatrix} \frac {\partial F}{\partial u} & \frac {\partial F}{\partial v}\\[1ex] \frac {\partial G}{\partial u} & \frac {\partial G}{\partial v} \end {vmatrix}. \] Similarly, for three functions of three variables, the third-order determinant \[ \frac {\partial (F,G, H)}{\partial (u,v,w)} = \begin {vmatrix} \frac {\partial F}{\partial u} & \frac {\partial F}{\partial v} & \frac {\partial F}{\partial w}\\[1ex] \frac {\partial G}{\partial u} & \frac {\partial G}{\partial v} & \frac {\partial G}{\partial w}\\[1ex] \frac {\partial H}{\partial u} & \frac {\partial H}{\partial v} & \frac {\partial H}{\partial w} \end {vmatrix} \] is the Jacobian of \(F\), \(G\), \(H\) with respect to \(u\), \(v\), \(w\).

Properties of Jacobians

Both properties follow from the determinant form of the general chain rule.

1.
Chain rule. If \(x\) and \(y\) are functions of \(u\) and \(v\), while \(u\) and \(v\) are functions of \(r\) and \(s\), then \[\frac {\partial (x,y)}{\partial (r,s)} = \frac {\partial (x,y)}{\partial (u,v)}\,\frac {\partial (u,v)}{\partial (r,s)} .\]
2.
Inverse. If \(u\) and \(v\) are functions of \(x\) and \(y\), and the equations can be solved for \(x\) and \(y\) in terms of \(u\) and \(v\), then \[\frac {\partial (u,v)}{\partial (x,y)}\,\frac {\partial (x,y)}{\partial (u,v)}=1 .\] This is property 1 with \((r,s) = (u,v)\): the composite of a mapping and its inverse is the identity, whose Jacobian is \(1\).

Example 1.12.2. If \(x = uv\) and \(y =\frac {u + v}{u -v}\), find \(\frac {\partial (u,v)}{\partial (x,y)}\).

Solution. Computing \(u_x\), \(u_y\), \(v_x\) and \(v_y\) directly would require solving for \(u\) and \(v\), which is awkward. The derivatives of \(x\) and \(y\) are easy, so compute the inverse Jacobian first: \[\frac {\partial (x,y)}{\partial (u,v)} = \begin {vmatrix} v & u\\[1ex] -\frac {2v}{(u-v)^2} & \frac {2u}{(u-v)^2} \end {vmatrix} = \frac {2uv}{(u-v)^2} + \frac {2uv}{(u-v)^2} = \frac {4uv}{(u -v)^2}.\] By property 2, \[\frac {\partial (u,v)}{\partial (x,y)} = \frac {(u -v)^2}{4uv},\] wherever \(uv\neq 0\).

Exercise 1.12.3. If \(u = xyz\), \(v = x^2 + y^2 + z^2\) and \(w = x+y+z\), find \(\frac {\partial (x,y,z)}{\partial (u,v,w)}\).

Answer: \(\frac {\partial (u,v,w)}{\partial (x,y,z)} = 2(x-y)(y-z)(x-z)\), so \(\frac {\partial (x,y,z)}{\partial (u,v,w)} = \frac {1}{2(x-y)(y-z)(x-z)}\) wherever \(x\), \(y\), \(z\) are distinct.

Example 1.12.4. Find the Jacobian \(\frac {\partial (u,v)}{\partial (r,\theta )}\), where \(u = x^2 - y^2\), \(v = 2xy\) and \(x = r\cos \theta \), \(y = r\sin \theta \).

Solution. By property 1, \begin {align*} \frac {\partial (u,v)}{\partial (r,\theta )} & = \frac {\partial (u,v)}{\partial (x,y)}\,\frac {\partial (x,y)}{\partial (r,\theta )} = \begin {vmatrix} 2x & -2y\\ 2y & 2x \end {vmatrix}\, \begin {vmatrix} \cos \theta & -r\sin \theta \\ \sin \theta & r\cos \theta \end {vmatrix}\\[1ex] & = (4x^2 + 4y^2)\cdot r = 4r^2\cdot r = 4r^3 . \end {align*}

As a check, \(u = r^2\cos 2\theta \) and \(v = r^2\sin 2\theta \) directly, and \(\begin {vmatrix} 2r\cos 2\theta & -2r^2\sin 2\theta \\ 2r\sin 2\theta & 2r^2\cos 2\theta \end {vmatrix} = 4r^3\).

Example 1.12.5. Interpret the Jacobian of the linear mapping \(u = 3x + y\), \(v = x - 2y\) geometrically.

Solution. \[\frac {\partial (u,v)}{\partial (x,y)} = \begin {vmatrix}3 & 1\\ 1 & -2\end {vmatrix} = -7 .\] The unit square with sides \(\langle 1,0\rangle \) and \(\langle 0,1\rangle \) is sent to the parallelogram with sides \(\langle 3,1\rangle \) and \(\langle 1,-2\rangle \), whose area is \(|3(-2) - 1\cdot 1| = 7\). So the mapping multiplies areas by \(|J| = 7\), and the negative sign records that it reverses orientation: a counter-clockwise boundary becomes a clockwise one. For a non-linear mapping the same is true of small regions near each point, which is why \(|J|\) appears as the scale factor in the change of variables formula for double integrals.
