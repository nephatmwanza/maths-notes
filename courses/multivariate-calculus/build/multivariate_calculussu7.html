1.7 Absolute Maximum and Minimum Values

For a function of one variable, a continuous function on a closed interval attains a maximum and a minimum. The analogue in the plane replaces the closed interval by a set that is closed (it contains all its boundary points) and bounded (it lies inside some disc).

Theorem 1.7.1 (Extreme value theorem). If \(f\) is continuous on a closed bounded set \(D\) in \(\mathbb {R}^2\), then \(f\) attains an absolute maximum value \(f(x_1,y_1)\) and an absolute minimum value \(f(x_2,y_2)\) at some points \((x_1,y_1)\) and \((x_2,y_2)\) of \(D\).

An extreme value attained at an interior point of \(D\) is also a local extreme value, so by Theorem 1.5.2 it occurs at a critical point. Every other extreme value is attained on the boundary. This gives a procedure.

Closed Interval Method

To find the absolute maximum and minimum of a continuous function \(f\) on a closed bounded set \(D\):

1.
Find the values of \(f\) at the critical points of \(f\) in the interior of \(D\).
2.
Find the extreme values of \(f\) on the boundary of \(D\).
3.
The largest of the values from steps 1 and 2 is the absolute maximum value, and the smallest is the absolute minimum value.

Step 2 usually reduces to one-variable problems, one for each piece of the boundary, or is done with Lagrange multipliers.

Example 1.7.2. Find the absolute maximum and minimum values of \(f(x,y) = x^2 - 2xy + 2y\) on the rectangle \(D = \{(x,y) \mid 0\leq x \leq 3,\ 0 \leq y \leq 2 \}\).

Solution. The polynomial \(f\) is continuous on the closed bounded rectangle, so the extreme values exist.

Interior. \(f_x = 2x -2y\) and \(f_y = -2x + 2\) vanish only at \((1,1)\), which lies inside \(D\), and \(f(1,1) = 1\).

Boundary. The boundary consists of the four segments \(L_1\) to \(L_4\).

\(-\)
On \(L_1\): \(y = 0\), \(0\leq x\leq 3\), and \(f = x^2\), which increases from \(0\) to \(9\).
\(-\)
On \(L_2\): \(x = 3\), \(0\leq y\leq 2\), and \(f = 9 - 4y\), which decreases from \(9\) to \(1\).
\(-\)
On \(L_3\): \(y = 2\), \(0\leq x\leq 3\), and \(f = x^2 - 4x + 4 = (x-2)^2\), with minimum \(0\) at \(x = 2\) and maximum \(4\) at \(x = 0\).
\(-\)
On \(L_4\): \(x = 0\), \(0\leq y\leq 2\), and \(f = 2y\), from \(0\) to \(4\).

Comparison. The candidates are \(1\) from the interior and \(0\), \(1\), \(4\), \(9\) from the boundary. The absolute maximum is \(f(3,0) = 9\), and the absolute minimum is \(0\), attained at \((0,0)\) and at \((2,2)\).

Example 1.7.3. Find the absolute extreme values of \(f(x,y) = x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 2y\) on the closed triangle with vertices \((0,0)\), \((4,0)\) and \((0,4)\).

Solution. Interior. \(f_x = 2x - 2 = 0\) and \(f_y = 2y - 2 = 0\) give \((1,1)\), inside the triangle, with \(f(1,1) = -2\).

Boundary. On \(y = 0\), \(0\leq x\leq 4\): \(f = x^2 - 2x\), with minimum \(-1\) at \(x = 1\) and values \(0\) and \(8\) at the ends. On \(x = 0\) the same by symmetry. On the hypotenuse \(y = 4 - x\), \(0\leq x\leq 4\): \[f = x^2 + (4-x)^2 - 2x - 2(4-x) = 2x^2 - 8x + 8 = 2(x-2)^2,\] with minimum \(0\) at \((2,2)\) and maximum \(8\) at the ends.

Comparison. The absolute minimum is \(-2\) at \((1,1)\), and the absolute maximum is \(8\), at \((4,0)\) and \((0,4)\).

Example 1.7.4. Find the absolute extreme values of \(f(x,y) = 2x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 1\) on the disc \(x^2 + y^2\leq 4\), using a Lagrange multiplier on the boundary.

Solution. Interior. \(f_x = 4x - 4\) and \(f_y = 2y\) vanish at \((1,0)\), inside the disc, where \(f = -1\).

Boundary. On \(x^2 + y^2 = 4\), the equations \(\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g\) are \(4x - 4 = 2\lambda x\) and \(2y = 2\lambda y\). The second gives \(y = 0\) or \(\lambda = 1\). If \(y = 0\), then \(x = \pm 2\), with \(f(2,0) = 1\) and \(f(-2,0) = 17\). If \(\lambda = 1\), the first gives \(4x - 4 = 2x\), so \(x = 2\) and \(y = 0\) again.

Comparison. The absolute maximum is \(17\), at \((-2,0)\), and the absolute minimum is \(-1\), at \((1,0)\).
