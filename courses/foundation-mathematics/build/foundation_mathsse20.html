20 Mathematical Induction

Mathematical induction deductive is a nature for it is usually to a tin conclusions. It is usually used in proving the variety of a statement involving all positive integer values of \(n\). Positive we divide a ladder we can do this if we know two things

\(1=1\hspace {1cm} =1^2\)

\(1+3=4\hspace {1cm} =2^2\)

\(1+3+5=9\hspace {1cm} =3^2\)

\(1+3+5+7=16\hspace {1cm} =4^2\)

Exmple
Prove by mathematical induction, that for all positive integer value of \(n\)

(a).
\(1+3+5+\cdots \cdots +(2n-1)=n^2\)

Proof.

Step 1:
We show that it is true for \(n=1\)

\(n=1\),  \(2(1)-1=1\):  R.H.S  \(1^2=1\)

\(n=1\),  \(2(1)-1=1=1^2\)

\(\therefore \)   It is true for \(n=1\).
Step 2:
We assume that it is true for \(n=k\).

\(1+3+5+\cdots +(2k-1)=k^2\). This is true by assumption.
Step 3:
We show that it is true for \(n=k+1\)

\(1+3+5+\cdots +(2k-1)+(2(k+1)-1)=(k+1)^2\)

Take the left hand side and show that it is equal to \((k+1)^2\).

By step 2,

\(1+3+5+\cdots +(2k-1)=k^2\)

Then
\( \implies \hspace {1cm} \begin {matrix} k^2+(2(k+1)-1) & =& k^2+2k+2-1\\ & = & k^2+2k+1\\ & = & (k+1)^2\\ \end {matrix} \)

\(\therefore \)   It is true for \(n=k+1\).

(b). \(4+8+12+\cdots +4n=2n(n+1)\) □

Proof.

Step 1:
We show that it is true for \(n=1\)

\(n=1\),  \(4(1)=2(1)+(1+1)=4\)

\(\therefore \)   it is true for \(n=1\).
Step 2:
We assume that it is true for \(n=k\).

\(4+8+12+\cdots +4k =2k(k+1).\)
Step 3:
We show that it is true for \(n=k+1\).

\(\underbrace {4+8+12+\cdots +4k}+4(k+1)=2(k+1)(k+2)\)
.   by step 2

\( \begin {matrix} 2k(k+1)+4(k+1) & = & 2(k+1)(k+2)\\ & =& 2(k+1)(k+2)\hspace {1cm} \text {R.H.S}\\ \end {matrix} \)

\(\therefore \)   It is true for \(n=k+1\).

(c). \(\dfrac {1}{2}+\dfrac {1}{2^2}+\dfrac {1}{2^3}+\cdots +\dfrac {1}{2^n}=1-\dfrac {1}{2^n}\) □

Proof.

Step 1:
We show that it is true for \(n=1\).

\(\dfrac {1}{2}=1-\dfrac {1}{2^1}=\dfrac {1}{2}\)

\(\therefore \)   It is true for \(n=1\).
Step 2:
We assume it is true for \(n=k\).

\(\dfrac {1}{2}+\dfrac {1}{2^2}+\dfrac {1}{2^3}+\cdots +\dfrac {1}{2^k}=1-\dfrac {1}{2^k}\)
Step 3:
We show that it is true for \(n=k+1\).

\(\dfrac {1}{2}+\dfrac {1}{2^2}+\dfrac {1}{2^3}+\cdots +\dfrac {1}{2^k}+\dfrac {1}{2^{k+1}}=1-\dfrac {1}{2^{k+1}}\)

We take the L.H.S and show that it is equal to R.H.S.

By step 2, we have that
\( \begin {matrix} 1-\dfrac {1}{2^k}+\dfrac {1}{2^{k+1}} & = & \dfrac {2\cdot 2^k-2+1}{2\cdot 2^k}\\\\ & = & \dfrac {2\cdot 2^k-1}{2\cdot 2^k}\\\\ & = & 1-\dfrac {1}{2\cdot 2^k}\\\\ & = & 1-\dfrac {1}{2^{k+1}}\\ \end {matrix} \)

\(\therefore \)   It is true for \(n=k+1\).

(d). \(x^n-y^n\) is divisible by \(x-y\) □

Proof.

Step 1:
We show that it is true for \(n=1\).

\(n=1,\)   \(x^1-y^1=x-y\) it is divisible by \(x-y\).

\(\therefore \)   It is true for \(n=1\).
Step 2:
We assume that it is true for \(n=k\).

\(\therefore \)   \(x^k-y^k\) is divisible by \(x-y\).
Step 3:
We show that \(x^{k+1}-y^{k+1}\) is divisible by \(x-y\).

\(x^kx-xy^k+xy^k-y^ky=x(x^k-y^k)+y^k(x-y)\)

The first part on R.H.S is divisible by \(x-y\) by step 2, and the second part on R.H.S is divisible by \(x-y\) because it has a factor \(x-y\).

□

Sample Questions

Prove by mathematical induction that for all positive integer values \(n\).

1.
\(1+4+7+\cdots +(3n-2)=\dfrac {n(3n-1)}{2}\)
2.
\(1^2+2^2+3^2+\cdots +n^2=\dfrac {n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}\)
3.
\(n^3+2n\) is divisible by 3.
4.
\((n+2)!>3^n\) for any integer \(n\geq 1\).
5.
\(n^3-n\) is divisible by 3. \(\forall n \in \mathbb {N}, n\geq 2\).
6.
\(1+2+2^2+\cdots +2^{n-1}=2^n-1\).
7.
\(6^n+4\) is divisible by 5, \(n\geq 1\).
8.
\(1+r+r^2+r^3+\cdots +r^n=\dfrac {1-r^{n+1}}{1-r},\hspace {0.4cm} r\neq 1\).
