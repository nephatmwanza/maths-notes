16.5 Derivatives of Logarithmic Function

\[\boxed {\dfrac {d}{dx}\, \log _a (x)=\dfrac {1}{x\, ln (a)}}\]

We can verify this differentiation formula.

Let \(\hspace {0.2cm}m=\log _a (x)\, \iff \, a^m = x\) \[\boxed {a^m = x\hspace {0.3cm}\cdots \hspace {0.3cm}(1)}\]

Now take the natural log on both sides of equation (1) \begin {align*} \ln (a^m) & = \ln (x)\\\\ \implies \hspace {0.3cm} m\, \ln (a) & = \ln (x)\\\\ \implies \hspace {0.3cm} m & = \dfrac {\ln (x)}{\ln (a)} \end {align*}

Therefore, \(\displaystyle {\log _a(x)= m = \dfrac {\ln (x)}{\ln (a)}}\)

Hence \(\displaystyle {\dfrac {d}{dx}\, \log _a(x) = \dfrac {1}{\ln (a)}\,\dfrac {d}{dx}\, \ln (x)}\)

\[\implies \hspace {0.3cm}\dfrac {d}{dx}\, \log _a(x) = \dfrac {1}{x\, \ln (a)}\]

Therefore, the general formula is \begin {align*} \dfrac {d}{dx}\, \log _a[f(x)] & = \dfrac {1}{\ln (a)}\, \dfrac {d}{dx}\, \ln [f(x)]\\\\ \implies \hspace {0.3cm}\dfrac {d}{dx}\, \log _a[f(x)] & = \dfrac {f'(x)}{f(x)\, \ln (a)}\\\\ \end {align*}

Example 16.10.

Find the derivative of \(\hspace {0.3cm}\displaystyle {y = \log _{10}(x^3 + 2x^2)}\).

Solution.

\(y = \log _{10}(x^3 + 2x^2)\)

Let \(f(x) = x^3 + 2x^2\, \implies \, f'(x) = 3x^2 + 4x\)

\begin {align*} \dfrac {d}{dx}\, \log _a [f(x)] & = \dfrac {f'(x)}{f(x) \, \ln (a)}\\\\ \implies \hspace {0.2cm} \dfrac {d}{dx}\, \log _{10}(x^3 + 2x^2) & = \dfrac {3x^2 + 4x}{(x^3 + 2x^2)\, \ln (10)}\\\\ \therefore \hspace {0.2cm} \dfrac {d}{dx}\, \log _{10}(x^3 + 2x^2) & = \dfrac {3x + 4}{(x^2 + 2x)\, \ln (10)}\\\\ \end {align*}
