4.17 Practice Problems

Problem 4.17.1. Let \(\underline {a} = \langle 1, 2, 2\rangle \) and \(\underline {b} = \langle 3, -4, 0\rangle \). Find the angle between them and the projection of \(\underline {a}\) on \(\underline {b}\).

Show solution

Solution. \[\underline {a}\cdot \underline {b} = 3 - 8 + 0 = -5,\qquad \left |\underline {a}\right | = \sqrt {1+4+4} = 3,\qquad \left |\underline {b}\right | = \sqrt {9+16} = 5 .\] Hence \[\cos \theta = \frac {\underline {a}\cdot \underline {b}} {\left |\underline {a}\right |\left |\underline {b}\right |} = \frac {-5}{15} = -\frac {1}{3} \implies \theta = \cos ^{-1}\Big (-\frac {1}{3}\Big ) \approx 109.5^{\circ }.\] The angle is obtuse, which is consistent with the dot product being negative. The projection is \[\text {proj}_{\underline {b}}\,\underline {a} = \frac {\underline {a}\cdot \underline {b}}{\left |\underline {b}\right |^2} \underline {b} = \frac {-5}{25}\langle 3,-4,0\rangle = \Big \langle -\frac {3}{5}, \frac {4}{5}, 0\Big \rangle .\]

Problem 4.17.2. Find the area of the triangle with vertices \(P(1,0,1)\), \(Q(2,3,0)\) and \(R(0,1,2)\).

Show solution

Solution. \[\overrightarrow {PQ} = \langle 1,3,-1\rangle ,\qquad \overrightarrow {PR} = \langle -1,1,1\rangle .\] \[\overrightarrow {PQ}\times \overrightarrow {PR} = \begin {vmatrix} \textbf {i} & \textbf {j} & \textbf {k}\\ 1 & 3 & -1\\ -1 & 1 & 1 \end {vmatrix} = \textbf {i}(3 + 1) - \textbf {j}(1 - 1) + \textbf {k}(1 + 3) = \langle 4, 0, 4\rangle .\] The area of the parallelogram is \(\left |\langle 4,0,4\rangle \right | = 4\sqrt 2\), so the triangle has area \[\frac {1}{2}\cdot 4\sqrt 2 = 2\sqrt 2 .\]

Problem 4.17.3. Find parametric and symmetric equations of the line through \((1,2,3)\) and \((4,-1,0)\).

Show solution

Solution. A direction vector is \[\overline {V} = \langle 4-1,\ -1-2,\ 0-3\rangle = \langle 3,-3,-3\rangle ,\] and since only its direction matters we may divide by \(3\) and use \(\langle 1,-1,-1\rangle \). Taking \((1,2,3)\) as the base point, the parametric equations are \[x = 1 + t,\qquad y = 2 - t,\qquad z = 3 - t ,\] and eliminating \(t\) gives the symmetric equations \[\frac {x-1}{1} = \frac {y-2}{-1} = \frac {z-3}{-1}.\]

Problem 4.17.4. Find the equation of the plane through \(P(1,0,1)\), \(Q(2,3,0)\) and \(R(0,1,2)\).

Show solution

Solution. A normal to the plane is any vector perpendicular to two vectors lying in it, and the cross product computed above serves: \[\underline {n} = \overrightarrow {PQ}\times \overrightarrow {PR} = \langle 4, 0, 4\rangle ,\] which may be divided by \(4\) to give \(\underline {n} = \langle 1,0,1\rangle \). The plane through \(P(1,0,1)\) with this normal is \[1(x - 1) + 0(y - 0) + 1(z - 1) = 0 \hspace {0.5cm}\implies \hspace {0.5cm} x + z = 2 .\] All three points satisfy this, as a check confirms.

Problem 4.17.5. Find the distance from the point \((2,5,4)\) to the plane \(x + z = 2\).

Show solution

Solution. For a plane \(ax + by + cz = d\) the distance from a point \((x_0,y_0,z_0)\) is \[D = \frac {\left |ax_0 + by_0 + cz_0 - d\right |}{\sqrt {a^2+b^2+c^2}} .\] Here \(a = 1\), \(b = 0\), \(c = 1\), \(d = 2\), so \[D = \frac {\left |2 + 0 + 4 - 2\right |}{\sqrt {1 + 0 + 1}} = \frac {4}{\sqrt 2} = 2\sqrt 2 .\] Note that \(y\) does not appear in the plane’s equation, and correspondingly the \(y-\)coordinate of the point has no effect on the distance: the plane is parallel to the \(y-\)axis.

Problem 4.17.6. Find the curvature of the circular helix \(\overline {r}(t) = \langle \cos t, \sin t, t\rangle \).

Show solution

Solution. \[\overline {r}\,'(t) = \langle -\sin t, \cos t, 1\rangle ,\qquad \overline {r}\,''(t) = \langle -\cos t, -\sin t, 0\rangle ,\] so \(\left |\overline {r}\,'\right | = \sqrt {\sin ^2t + \cos ^2t + 1} = \sqrt 2\), a constant — the helix is traced at constant speed. Now \[\overline {r}\,'\times \overline {r}\,'' = \begin {vmatrix} \textbf {i} & \textbf {j} & \textbf {k}\\ -\sin t & \cos t & 1\\ -\cos t & -\sin t & 0 \end {vmatrix} = \langle \sin t,\ -\cos t,\ 1\rangle ,\] whose magnitude is \(\sqrt {\sin ^2t + \cos ^2t + 1} = \sqrt 2\). Therefore \[K = \frac {\left |\overline {r}\,'\times \overline {r}\,''\right |} {\left |\overline {r}\,'\right |^3} = \frac {\sqrt 2}{\big (\sqrt 2\big )^3} = \frac {1}{2}.\] The curvature is constant, as symmetry demands: every point of a helix looks like every other.
