6.4 Partitioned regression: the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem

Partition the regressors into two groups, \(X = [X_1\ \ X_2]\), with \(k_1\) and \(k_2\) columns, and correspondingly \(b = (b_1', b_2')'\). Let \(M_1 = I - X_1(X_1'X_1)^{-1}X_1'\) be the residual maker for \(X_1\) alone.

Theorem 6.4.1 (Frisch–Waugh–Lovell). The subvector \(b_2\) of the least squares estimates in the regression of \(Y\) on \([X_1\ \ X_2]\) is \[ b_2 = (X_2'M_1X_2)^{-1}X_2'M_1Y . \] Equivalently, \(b_2\) is obtained by regressing the residuals \(M_1Y\) (of \(Y\) on \(X_1\)) on the residuals \(M_1X_2\) (of each column of \(X_2\) on \(X_1\)). The residuals of that regression are identical to the residuals \(e\) of the full regression.

Proof. The full residual vector satisfies \(Y = X_1b_1 + X_2b_2 + e\) with \(X_1'e = 0\) and \(X_2'e = 0\). Premultiply by \(M_1\). Since \(M_1X_1 = 0\), and \(M_1e = e - X_1(X_1'X_1)^{-1}X_1'e = e\), \[ M_1Y = M_1X_2b_2 + e . \] Premultiply by \(X_2'M_1 = (M_1X_2)'\) and use \(X_2'M_1e = X_2'e = 0\): \(X_2'M_1Y = X_2'M_1X_2b_2\). The matrix \(X_2'M_1X_2\) is non-singular because \(X\) has full rank, which gives the formula. Finally \(e = M_1Y - M_1X_2b_2\) is exactly the residual vector of the regression of \(M_1Y\) on \(M_1X_2\), since \((M_1X_2)'e = 0\) shows that \(b_2\) satisfies that regression’s normal equations. □

The theorem is the precise meaning of “holding the other variables constant”. A multiple regression coefficient measures the relation between \(Y\) and \(X_2\) after the linear influence of \(X_1\) has been removed from both. Nothing is literally held fixed; the effect of \(X_1\) is partialled out. Two special cases are used constantly.

Corollary 6.4.2.

(i)
(Deviation form.) Take \(X_1 = \iota \), so \(M_1 = M_0\) and \(M_0X_2\), \(M_0Y\) are deviations from means. The slope coefficients are obtained by regressing the deviations of \(Y\) on the deviations of the regressors, \(b_2 = (X_2'M_0X_2)^{-1}X_2'M_0Y\), and the intercept is \(b_1 = \bar Y - b_2\bar X_2 - \dots - b_k\bar X_k\). With two regressors in deviation form, \[ b_2 = \frac {\sum x_2y\sum x_3^2 - \sum x_3y\sum x_2x_3}{\sum x_2^2\sum x_3^2 - \bigl (\sum x_2x_3\bigr )^2}, \qquad b_3 = \frac {\sum x_3y\sum x_2^2 - \sum x_2y\sum x_2x_3}{\sum x_2^2\sum x_3^2 - \bigl (\sum x_2x_3\bigr )^2} . \]
(ii)
(Detrending.) Including a time trend in a regression gives the same slope coefficients as first removing a linear trend from every variable and then regressing the detrended series on one another.

Proof. (i) is the theorem with \(X_1 = \iota \); the explicit formulas are the inverse of the \(2 \times 2\) matrix of deviation sums of squares and cross-products, as in Example 6.2.3. (ii) is the theorem with \(X_1 = [\iota \ \ t]\). □

Part (ii) was Frisch and Waugh’s original result in 1933, and it answered a debate of the time about whether economic series should be detrended before a regression is run. The answer is that it makes no difference to the slopes, provided the trend is in the regression.
