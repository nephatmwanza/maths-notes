4.6 Maximum likelihood for the autoregressive equation

Suppose now that the \(u_t\) are independent \(N(0, \sigma ^2)\) and the observations are \(Y_1, \dots , Y_T\). The joint density factors as \(f(Y_1)\prod _{t=2}^T f(Y_t \mid Y_{t-1})\), and given \(Y_{t-1}\), \(Y_t \sim N(\alpha + \beta Y_{t-1}, \sigma ^2)\).

Theorem 4.6.1.

(i)
The conditional log-likelihood, which treats \(Y_1\) as fixed, is \[ \ell _c(\alpha , \beta , \sigma ^2) = -\frac {T-1}{2}\log (2\pi \sigma ^2) - \frac {1}{2\sigma ^2}\sum _{t=2}^T (Y_t - \alpha - \beta Y_{t-1})^2 , \] and it is maximised by the least squares estimates of \(\alpha \) and \(\beta \), with \(\hat \sigma ^2 = \text {RSS}/(T - 1)\).
(ii)
If \(|\beta | < 1\) and the process is stationary, \(Y_1 \sim N\bigl (\alpha /(1 - \beta ),\ \sigma ^2/(1 - \beta ^2)\bigr )\), and the exact log-likelihood is \[ \ell (\alpha , \beta , \sigma ^2) = \ell _c - \frac 12\log \frac {2\pi \sigma ^2}{1 - \beta ^2} - \frac {1 - \beta ^2}{2\sigma ^2}\left (Y_1 - \frac {\alpha }{1 - \beta }\right )^2 . \] Its maximiser is not least squares and must be found numerically.

Proof. (i) The conditional density of each \(Y_t\) is normal with mean \(\alpha + \beta Y_{t-1}\) and variance \(\sigma ^2\); the log of the product of \(T - 1\) such densities is \(\ell _c\). For any \(\sigma ^2\), \(\ell _c\) is maximised over \((\alpha , \beta )\) by minimising the sum of squares, which is least squares. Setting \(\partial \ell _c/\partial \sigma ^2 = -\frac {T-1}{2\sigma ^2} + \frac {\text {RSS}}{2\sigma ^4} = 0\) gives \(\hat \sigma ^2 = \text {RSS}/(T - 1)\).

(ii) Add \(\log f(Y_1)\) for the stated normal distribution, which is Theorem 4.5.2 with normal shocks. The extra terms involve \(\beta \) non-linearly through \(1 - \beta ^2\), so the first-order conditions are no longer linear. □

The two estimators differ only through one observation’s contribution out of \(T\), so they have the same large-sample distribution. In small samples the exact likelihood uses the information in \(Y_1\), and it automatically keeps \(|\hat \beta | < 1\), since the term \(\log (1 - \beta ^2)\) tends to \(-\infty \) as \(|\beta | \to 1\). The general theory of maximum likelihood, which shows that such estimators are consistent, asymptotically normal and efficient, is developed in its own section.
