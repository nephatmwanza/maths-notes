5.6 Confidence Regions

\(\bullet \)
Here we extended the concept of the unvariate confidence interval to a multivariate confidence region.
\(\bullet \)
The region is determined by the data and for the moment, denotes it by \(R(X)\), where \(X= \begin {pmatrix} \underline {X}_1,\underline {X}_2,\cdots \cdots ,\underline {X}_n\\ \end {pmatrix}\)is the data matrix.
\(\bullet \)
The region \(R(X)\) is said to the \(100(1-\alpha )\%\) confidence region if before the sample is selected.

\(Pr( R(X)\) will cover the true \(\underline {\theta })=1-\alpha \hspace {0.3cm} \cdots \cdots \cdots \cdots \hspace {0.3cm} (1)\)

\(\bullet \)
This \(Pr\) is calculated under the true but unknown value of \(\theta \).
\(\bullet \)
The confidence region for \(\underline {\mu }\) of \(P-\) dimensional normal population from our previous work satisfies. \[Pr\Bigg [n\big (\overline {\underline {X}}-\underline {\mu }\big )'S^{-1}\big (\overline {\underline {X}}-\underline {\mu }\big )\leq \dfrac {(n-1)p}{n-p}F_{p,n-p}(\alpha )\Bigg ] = 1 - \alpha \hspace {0.3cm} \cdots \cdots \cdots \cdots \hspace {0.3cm} (2)\]
\(\bullet \)
For a particular sample \(\overline {\underline {X}}\) and \(S\) can be computed and the inequality \[ n\big (\overline {\underline {X}}-\underline {\mu }\big )'S^{-1}\big (\overline {\underline {X}}-\underline {\mu }\big )\leq \dfrac {(n-1)p}{n-p}F_{p,n-p}(\alpha )\] will define a region \(R(X)\) and this particular region is an ellipsoid centered at \(\overline {\underline {X}}\).

Definition 5.23. A \(100(1-\alpha )\%\) confidence region for the mean of \(p-\) dimensional normal distribution is the set determined by all \(\underline {\mu }\) such that \[n\big (\overline {\underline {X}}-\underline {\mu }\big )'S^{-1}\big (\overline {\underline {X}}-\underline {\mu }\big )\leq \frac {p(n-1)}{n-p}F_{p,n-p,1-\alpha }\] where \(\overline {\underline {X}}\) and \(S\) are the usual sample quantities.

Beginning at the center \(\overline {\underline {X}}\) the axes of the confidence ellipsoid are \[\pm \sqrt {\lambda _i}\sqrt {\dfrac {p(n-1)}{n(n-p)}F_{p,n-p,(1-\alpha )}}\underline {e}_i\] where \(S\underline {e}_i = \lambda _i\underline {e}_ip\hspace {0.3cm} , \hspace {0.5cm} i= (1)p\).

Simultaneous Confidence Statements

The confidence region in (3) \(\displaystyle { n\big (\overline {\underline {X}}-\underline {\mu }\big )'S^{-1}\big (\overline {\underline {X}}-\underline {\mu }\big ) \leq C^2}\hspace {0.2cm}\) where \(C\) is constant. Correctly asses the joint knowledge concern values for \(\underline {\mu }\). There is need, however to make statements about the individual components means or specific linear combinations.

Let \(\underline {X}\thicksim N_p\big (\underline {\mu },\Sigma \big )\) and consider the linear combinations. \[Z = L_1X_1 + L_2X_2 + \cdots \cdots \cdots + L_pX_p\hspace {0.3cm} \cdots \cdots \cdots \hspace {0.3cm} (4)\] From the previous work, we know that \[\mu _Z =E(Z) = E\big (\underline {L}'\underline {X}\big ) = \underline {L}'E(\underline {X})=L'\underline {\mu }\hspace {0.3cm} \cdots \cdots \cdots \hspace {0.3cm} (5)\] and \(\hspace {0.2cm} \displaystyle {var(Z) = var\big (\underline {L}'\underline {X}\big ) = \underline {L}'var(\underline {X})\underline {L}=\underline {L}'\Sigma \underline {L}\hspace {0.3cm} \cdots \cdots \cdots \hspace {0.3cm} (6)}\)

Since \(Z\) is a linear combination of normal variance, \(Z\) itself has a normal distribution. \[Z\thicksim N\big (\underline {L}'\underline {\mu }, \underline {L}'\Sigma \underline {L}\big ) \hspace {0.3cm} \cdots \cdots \cdots \hspace {0.3cm} (7)\]

Once sample values are available, we can estimate \(\mu _Z= \underline {L}'\underline {\mu }\) and \(\Sigma _Z = \underline {L}'\Sigma \underline {L}\) in that case sample estimate become \[\overline {Z} = \underline {L}'\overline {\underline {X}}\hspace {0.3cm} \cdots \cdots \cdots \hspace {0.3cm} (8)\] \(S^2_2 = \displaystyle {\underline {L}'S\underline {L}}\), where \(S\) is the usual sample covariance matrix.

For the fixed \(L\), and \(\sigma ^2_Z\) unknown a \(100(1-\alpha )\%\) confidence interval for \(\mu _Z = \underline {L}'\underline {\mu }\) based on student’s \(t\) ratio \[t = \dfrac {\overline {Z}-\mu _Z}{S_Z/\sqrt {n}}=\dfrac {\sqrt {n}\big (\underline {L}'\overline {\underline {X}}-\underline {L}'\underline {\mu }\big )}{\sqrt {\underline {L}'S\underline {L}}}\hspace {0.3cm} \cdots \cdots \cdots \hspace {0.3cm} (9)\] and leads to the statement \(M.E=\) Margin of Error

\[\overline {Z}-t_{n-1,(1-\alpha /2)}\cdot \frac {S_Z}{\sqrt {n}}\leq \mu _Z \leq \overline {Z}+t_{n-1,(1-\alpha /2}\cdot \frac {S_Z}{\sqrt {n}}\]

or

\[\underline {L}'\overline {\underline {X}}-t_{n-1, (1-\alpha /2)} \sqrt {\dfrac {\underline {L}'S\underline {L}}{n}} \leq L'\mu \leq \underline {L}'\overline {\underline {X}} + t_{n-1,(1-\alpha /2)}\frac {\sqrt {\underline {L}'S\underline {L}'}}{\sqrt {n}}\hspace {0.2cm} \cdots \cdots \cdots \hspace {0.3cm} (10)\]

The \(T^2-\) Confidence Intervals

It can be shown that \(T^2=n\big (\overline {\underline {X}}-\underline {\mu }\big )'S^{-1}\big (\overline {\underline {X}}-\underline {\mu }\big )\leq C^2\) implies that \(\dfrac {n\big (L'\overline {\underline {X}}-L'\underline {\mu }\big )^2}{L'SL}\leq C^2\hspace {0.3cm} \forall L\) or \[L'\overline {\underline {X}}-C\sqrt {\dfrac {L'SL}{n}}\leq \underline {L}'\underline {\mu }\leq L'\underline {\overline {X}}+C\sqrt {\dfrac {L'SL}{n}}\hspace {0.3cm} \cdots \cdots \cdots \hspace {0.3cm}(*)\]

and choosing \(C^2= \dfrac {p(n-1)}{n-p}F_{p,n-p}(1-\alpha )\), then the interval \[L'\overline {\underline {X}}- \sqrt {\dfrac {p(n-1)}{n-p}F_{p,n-p,(1-\alpha )}\dfrac {L'SL}{n}}\leq \underline {L}'\underline {\mu }\leq L'\overline {\underline {X}}+\sqrt {\dfrac {p(n-1)}{n-p}F_{p,n-p,(1-\alpha )}\dfrac {L'SL}{n}}\hspace {0.3cm}\cdots \cdots \cdots \hspace {0.3cm}(11) \] a \(100(1-\alpha )\%\) interval in (11) may be refereed as \(T\) interval since coverage probability for various choices may be determined by the Hotelling’s \(T^2\) distribution associated with \(F\) distribution.

Individual Mean Confidence Intervals

Various choices of \(L\) lead to intervals of individual component means \begin {align*} L'_1 & = \begin {pmatrix} 1 & , & 0 & , & 0 &, &\cdots \cdots & 0\\ \end {pmatrix}\\ L'_2 & = \begin {pmatrix} 0 & , & 1 & , & 0 & ,& \cdots \cdots & 0\\ \end {pmatrix}\\ \vdots & \\ L'_p & = \begin {pmatrix} 0 & , & 0 & , & 0 &, & \cdots \cdots & 1\\ \end {pmatrix}\\ \end {align*}

\begin {align*} L_1 & : \overline {X}_1-\sqrt {\dfrac {p(n-1)}{n-p}F_{p,n-p,1-\alpha }\dfrac {S_{11}}{n}}\leq \mu _1\leq \overline {X}_1+ \sqrt {\dfrac {p(n-1)}{n-p}F_{p,n-p,1-\alpha }\dfrac {S_{11}}{n}}\\\\ L_2 & : \overline {X}_2-\sqrt {\dfrac {p(n-1)}{n-p}F_{p,n-p,1-\alpha }\dfrac {S_{22}}{n}}\leq \mu _2\leq \overline {X}_2+ \sqrt {\dfrac {p(n-1)}{n-p}F_{p,n-p,1-\alpha }\dfrac {S_{22}}{n}}\\ & \vdots \\ & \vdots \\ L_p & : \overline {X}_p-\sqrt {\dfrac {p(n-1)}{n-p}F_{p,n-p,1-\alpha }\dfrac {S_{pp}}{n}}\leq \mu _2\leq \overline {X}_2+ \sqrt {\dfrac {p(n-1)}{n-p}F_{p,n-p,1-\alpha }\dfrac {S_{pp}}{n}}\\ \end {align*}
