2.5 Continuity and Connectedness

Theorem 2.5.1. Let \(f\) be a continuous function from \(X\left (\subset \mathbb {R}\right )\) into \(Y\left (\subset \mathbb {R}\right )\) and \(E\) is a connected subset of \(X\). Thne \(f(E)\) is connected in \(Y\).

Proof. Assume, on the contrary, that \(f(E)\) is not connected i.e \(f(E) = A\cup B\) where \(A\) and \(B\) are non-empty, separated subsets of \(Y\). Putting \(G = E \cap f^{-1}(A)\) and \(H = E\cap f^{-1}(B)\), we have \(E = G \cup H\), and neither \(G\) nor \(H\) is empty. Since \(A\subset \overline {A}\,,\,\) we have \(G\subset f^{-1}\left (\overline {A}\right )\). \(f^{-1}\left (\overline {A}\right )\) is closed since \(f\) is continuous. Hence \(\overline {G} \subset f^{-1}\left (\overline {A}\right )\). Thus, \(f\left (\overline {G}\right ) \subset \overline {A}\). Since \(f(H) = B\) and \(\overline {A} \cap B = \emptyset \), we conclude that \(\overline {G}\cap H = \emptyset \). The same argument shows that \(\overline {H}\cap G = \emptyset \). Thus \(G\) and \(H\) are separated. This contradicts that \(E\) is connected. Therefore \(f(E)\) is connected. □
