5.2 Properties of the Riemann Integral

We now consider Riemann integrable functions on a closed bounded interval \([a,b]\) and establish some of their properties.

We shall denote the set of all Riemann integrable functions on \([a,b]\) by \(R[a,b]\).

That is , we shall write \(f\in R[a,b]\) to mean that \(f\) is Riemann integrable on \([a,b]\) or \(f\) belongs to the class of Riemann integrable functions on \([a,b]\).

Theorem 5.2.1. If \(f\in R[a,b]\) and \(C\) is any real number, then \(C\, f\in R[a,b]\) and \[\int ^b_aCf \, = \, C\, \int ^b_af.\]

Proof. Since \(f\in R[a,b]\) then \(\overline {\int ^b_a}f\, = \,\underline {\int ^b_a}f\) and \(f\) is bounded on \([a,b]\). Now \(|Cf| = |C|\,|f|\) which implies that \(Cf\) is bounded on \([a,b]\). Now, for \(C = 0\), the result is obvious.

If \(C>0\), let \(P\) be a partition of \([a,b]\). Then \begin {align*} U(P,Cf) & = \sum ^n_{k = 1}C\, M_k(f)\, \Delta x_k\\ & = C\, \sum ^n_{k = 1} M_k(f)\, \Delta x_k\\ & = C\, U(P,f) \end {align*}

Thus \(\inf U(P,Cf) = C \inf U(P, f)\), and also \(\sup L(P, Cf) = C \sup L(P,f)\). Therefore \[\overline {\int ^b_a}Cf = \inf (U(P,Cf)) = C\, \inf U(P,f) = C\, \overline {\int ^b_a}f\] Since \(f\) in Riemann integrable, we have that \begin {align*} C\,\overline {\int ^b_a}f = C\,\underline {\int ^b_a}f & = C\, \sup L(P,f) = \sup L (P, Cf) = \underline {\int ^b_a}Cf \end {align*}

Thus \(\overline {\int ^b_a}Cf = \underline {\int ^b_a}Cf\) and \(Cf\in R[a,b]\).

Since \(\overline {\int ^b_a}Cf = C\,\underline {\int ^b_a}f\) we have \(\int ^b_aCf \, = \, C\, \int ^b_af\).

If \(C< 0\), then \(C\, M_k(f) = \inf \{C\,f(x):\, x\in [x_{k-1},x_k]\}\) and

\(C\, m_k(f) = \sup \{C\,f(x):\, x\in [x_{k-1},x_k]\}\) and so

\(\sup \{U(P,Cf):\, P\in P[a,b]\} = C\, \inf \{U(P,f):\, P\in P[a,b]\}\) and

\(\inf \{L(P,Cf):\, P\in P[a,b]\} = C\, \sup \{L(P,f):\, P\in P[a,b]\}.\) Therefore \begin {align*} \overline {\int ^b_a} Cf & = \inf \{L(P,Cf):\, P\in P[a,b]\} = C\, \sup \{L(P,f):\, P\in P[a,b]\}\\ & = C\, \underline {\int ^b_a}f = C\, \int ^b_af = C\, \overline {\int ^b_a}f\\ & = C\, \inf \{U(P,f):\, P\in P[a,b]\} = \sup \{U(P,Cf):\, P\in P[a,b]\}\\ & = \underline {\int ^b_a}Cf \end {align*}

Hence again \(Cf\in R[a,b]\) and \(\int ^b_a Cf\, = \, C\, \int ^b_af\). □

Theorem 5.2.2. If \(f\in R[a,b]\) and \(c\in [a,b]\) then \(f\in R[a,c]\) and \(f\in R[c,b]\) and conversely. In this case \[\int ^b_af \, = \,\int ^c_af \, + \, \int ^b_cf.\]

Proof. For \(c\in [a,b]\) we have that \(f\) is bounded on \([a,c]\) and on \([c,b]\) if and only if it is bounded on \([a,b]\). Since \(f\in R[a,b]\) given \(\varepsilon >0\) there exists a partition \(P\) such that \[U(P,f) - L(P,f) < \varepsilon \] Let \(P^* = P\cup \{c\}\) be a refinement of \(P\) by adding a point, then \(P^*\in [a,b]\) and \[U(P^*,f) - L(P^*,f)\leq U(P,f) - L(P,f) < \varepsilon \] We break the partition \(P^*\) on \([a,b]\) to \(P_1\) on \([a,c]\) and \(P_2\) on \([c,b]\). Then we have \begin {align*} U(P^*,f) - L(P^*,f) & = U(P_1,f) + U(P_2,f) - L(P_1,f) - L(P_2,f)\\ & = (U(P_1,f) - L(P_1,f)) + (U(P_2,f) - L(P_2,f))\\ & < \varepsilon \end {align*}

It follows that \(U(P_1,f) - L(P_1,f) < \varepsilon \) and \(U(P_2,f) - L(P_2,f) < \varepsilon .\) Thus \(f\in R[a,c]\) and \(f\in R[c,b]\).

Now it can be shown that \(U(P_1,f) + U(P_2,f) = U(P^*,f)\) implies that \[\inf \, U(P_1,f) \, + \, \inf \, U(P_2,f)\, = \, \inf \, U(P^*,f)\] This then implies that \(\int ^c_af\, + \, \int ^b_cf \, = \, \int ^b_af.\)

Conversely, in \(c\in (a,b)\) and \(f\in R[a,c]\) and \(f\in R[c,d]\), then there exists partitions \(P_2\in P[a,c]\) and \(P_2\in P[c,b]\) such that \(U(P_1,f) - L(P_1,f) < \frac {\varepsilon }{2}\) and \(U(P_2,f) - L(P_1,f)<\frac {\varepsilon }{2}\). Then for \(P= P_1\cup P_2\) we have \begin {align*} U(P,f) - L(P,f) & = U(P_1,f) + U(P_2,f) - L(P_1,f) - L(P_2,f)\\ & = U(P_1,f) - L(P_1,f) + U(P_2,f) - L(P_2,f)\\ & < \varepsilon \end {align*}

Hence \(f\in R[a,b]\) and the required relation of integrals follow as before. □

Corollary 5.2.3. If \(f\in [a,b]\) and \([c,c]\subset [a,b]\) then \(f\in R[c,d]\). That is if \(f\) is Riemann integrable on a closed bounded interval, then it is Riemann integrable on every closed subinterval.

Proof. Let \(\varepsilon >0\). Since \(f\in R[a,b]\), Riemann’s criterion gives a partition \(P\) of \([a,b]\) with \(U(f,P)-L(f,P)<\varepsilon \). Refine \(P\) by adjoining the points \(c\) and \(d\); refinement never increases the difference, so the refined partition \(P'\) also satisfies \(U(f,P')-L(f,P')<\varepsilon \).

Let \(Q\) be the part of \(P'\) lying in \([c,d]\), a partition of \([c,d]\). Every term of \(U(f,Q)-L(f,Q)\) appears among the terms of \(U(f,P')-L(f,P')\), and all terms are non-negative, so \[U(f,Q)-L(f,Q) \leq U(f,P')-L(f,P') < \varepsilon .\] By Riemann’s criterion again, \(f\in R[c,d]\). □

Theorem 5.2.4. Suppose that \(f,\, g\in R[a,b]\), then \(f + g\in R[a,b]\) and \[\int ^b_a(f + g) \, = \, \int ^b_af \, + \, \int ^b_ag\]

Proof. Since \(f,\, g\in R[a,b]\), then there functions are bounded on \([a,b]\). Therefore there exists on \(M>0\) such that \(|f(x)| \leq M\) and \(|g(x)| \leq M\) for all \(x\in [a,b]\). Thus \[|f(x) + g(x)| \leq |f(x)| + |g(x)|\leq 2M\] for all \(x\in [a,b]\). This shows that \(f + g\) is bounded on \([a,b]\). Therefore \(\overline {\int ^b_a}(f + g)\) and \(\underline {\int ^b_a}(f + g)\) exists.

But if \(A\subseteq [a,b]\) then we have \(\{f(x) + g(x):\, x\in A\} \subset \{f(x_1) + g(x_2):\, x_1,\, x_2\in A\}\). Thus for any partition \(P\) of \([a,b]\), we have \(U(P,f+g)\leq U(P,f) + U(P,g)\) and \(L(P,f+g)\geq L(P,f) + L(P,g)\). Consequently, \begin {align*} \sup L(P,f) + \sup L (P,g) & \leq \sup L(P, f+g) \\ & \leq \inf U(P,f+g) \\ & \leq \inf U(P,f) + \inf U(P,g) \end {align*}

Thus \[\underline {\int ^b_a}f\, + \, \underline {\int ^b_a}g \, \leq \, \underline {\int ^b_a}(f + g)\, \leq \overline {\int ^b_a}(f+ g)\, \leq \,\overline {\int ^b_a}f\, + \, \overline {\int ^b_a}g\] and since \(f\) and \(g\) are Riemann integrable on \([a,b]\), we have \[\underline {\int ^b_a}f = \int ^b_af = \overline {\int ^b_a}f \qquad \text {and}\qquad \underline {\int ^b_a}g = \int ^b_a g = \overline {\int ^b_a}g,\] so that \[\int ^b_a f\, + \, \int ^b_a g\, \leq \, \int ^b_a(f + g)\, \leq \,\overline {\int ^b_a}(f + g)\, \leq \, \int ^b_af \, + \, \int ^b_a g\] This shows that all the inequalities are in fact equalities. Hence \(f + g \in R[a,b]\) and \[\int ^b_a (f + g) \, = \, \overline {\int ^b_a}(f + g) \, = \, \int ^b_a f \, + \, \int ^b_a g.\] □

Theorem 5.2.5. Let \(f,g\in R[a,b]\) and \(f\leq g\) on \([a,b]\), then \[\int ^b_a \leq \int ^b_ag.\]

Proof. For any partition \(P\) of \([a,b]\) we have \(L(P,f)\leq U(P,f) \leq U(P,g).\) This implies that \begin {align*} &\sup _P\{L(P,f) - U(P,g)\} \leq 0\\ \implies \quad & \sup _P\, L(P,f) - \inf \, U(P,g) \leq 0\\ \implies \quad &\int ^b_a f\, - \, \int ^b_a g \leq 0\\ \implies \quad & \int ^b_af \leq \int ^b_a g \end {align*} □

Theorem 5.2.6. Suppose that \(f,\, g\in R[a,b]\) then \(fg\in R[a,b]\).

Proof. Since \(f, \, g\in R[a,b]\), they are bounded on \([a,b]\). Therefore, there exists \(K> 0\) such that \(|fg| = |f|\, |g|\, = \, K^2\) on \([a,b]\).

This implies that \(fg\) is also bounded on \([a,b]\). Thus for any partition \(P\) of \([a,b]\) both \(U(P,fg)\) and \(L(P,fg)\) exists and are bounded We also have that for any \(\varepsilon >0\) there exists a partition \(P\) of \([a,b]\) such that \(U(P,f) - L(P,f) <\varepsilon \) and \(U(P,g) - L(P,g)<\varepsilon \).

Let \(M_r(f)\, ,\, m_r(f)\) be bounds of \(f\) on \([x_{r-1},x_r]\)

\(M_r(g)\, ,\, m_r(g)\) be bounds of \(g\) on \([x_{r-1},x_r]\)

\(M_r(fg)\, ,\, m_r(fg)\) be bounds of \(fg\) on \([x_{r-1},x_r]\)

Then, for all \(x',\, x''\in [x_{r - 1}, x_r]\) we have \begin {align*} |fg(x'') - fg(x')| & = |g(x'')\, f(x'') - f(x') + f(x')\,g(x'') - g(x')|\\ & \leq |g(x'')|\, |f(x'') - f(x')| + |f(x')|\, |g(x'') - g(x')|\\ &\leq K\, |M_r(f) - m_r(f)| + K\, |M_r(g) - m_r(g)| \end {align*}

Thus \((M_r(fg) - m_r(fg))\leq K(M_r(f) - m_r(f)) + K(M_r(g) - m_r(g)).\) Hence \begin {align*} \sum ^n_{r=1}(M_r(fg) - m_r(fg))\, \Delta x_r &\leq K\sum ^n_{r=1}(M_r(f) - m_r(f))\, \Delta x_r \\ &\qquad + K\sum ^n_{r=1}(M_r(g) - m_r(g))\, \Delta x_r \end {align*}

Thus \(U(P,fg) - L(P,fg) < K\, \varepsilon + K\, \varepsilon = 2K\varepsilon \,.\) This implies that \(fg\in R[a,b]\). □

Theorem 5.2.7. Let \(f,\, g\in R[a,b]\) and suppose that there exists \(t>0\) such that \(|g| > t\) on \([a,b]\), then \(\frac {f}{g}\in R[a,b]\).

Proof. The proof is similar to that of Theorem 5.2.6, but in this case \(\left |\frac {f}{g}\right |\leq \frac {K}{t}\) on \([a,b]\) and if \(M_r\left (\frac {f}{g}\right )\, ,\, m_r\left (\frac {f}{g}\right )\) denotes the bounds of \(\frac {f}{g}\) on \([x_{r-1}, x_r]\) then \[M_r\left (\frac {f}{g}\right ) - m_r\left (\frac {f}{g}\right ) \leq \dfrac {K}{t^2}\, (M_r(f) - m_r(f)) \, + \, \dfrac {K}{t^2}\, (M_r(g) - m_r(g)).\] □

Theorem 5.2.8. If \(f\in R[a,b]\) then \(|f| \in R[a,b]\) and \(\left |\displaystyle {\int ^b_a f}\right |\, \leq \, \int ^b_a|f|\).

Proof. Since \(f\in R[a,b],],\) there exists \(K\) such that \(|f| < K\) on \([a,b]\) and so \(|f|\) is also bounded on \([a,b]\). Also given \(\varepsilon > 0\) there exist a partition \(P\) of \([a,b]\) such that \(U(P,f) - L(P,f) < \varepsilon \).

Let \(M_r(f)\, , \, m_r(f)\, ,\, M_r(|f|)\) and \(m_r(|f|)\) so the bounds of \(f\) and \(|f|\) respectively on \([x_{r-1},x_r]\), then for all \(x'\, , \, x''_r\in [x_{r-1},x_r]\) we have \(||f(x'')| - |f(x')|| \leq |f(x'') - f(x')|\) and by taking supremum we have \(M_r(|f|) - m_r(|f|) \leq M_r(f) - m_r(f)\,.\) Thus \begin {align*} U(P,|f|) - L(P,|f|) & = \sum ^n_{r=1}(M_r(|f|) - m_r(|f|))\, \Delta x_r\\ & \leq \sum ^n_{r=1}(M_r(f) - m_r(f))\, \Delta x_r\\ & = U(P,f) - L(L,f)\\ & < \varepsilon \end {align*}

Hence \(|f|\in R[a,b]\) since \(|f|> f\) on \([a,b]\) we have \[\left |\displaystyle {\sum ^n_{r=1}M_r(f)\, \Delta x_r}\right |\leq \sum ^n_{r=1}M_r \, |f|\, \Delta x_r\] That is \(|U(P,f)|\leq U(P,f)\) for all the partition \(P\in P[a,b]\). Let \(\|P\| \longrightarrow 0\) to get \[\left |\displaystyle {\int ^b_a f}\right |\, \leq \, \int ^b_a |f|\] □

Remark. If \(f\in R[a,b]\) and \(M\) and \(m\) are bounds of \(f\) on \([a,b]\), then \[m\,(b - a)\leq \int ^b_af \leq M\, (b-a)\]

Proof. Since \(m\leq f \leq M\) on \([a,b]\) , then for \(f\in R[a,b]\) we have \[\int ^b_a m \leq \int ^b_a f \leq \int ^b_a M\]

\[\implies \quad m\,(b - a) \leq \int ^b_a f\leq M\,(b-a)\] These inequalities also follow from the fact that \[m\, (b - a) \leq L(P , f) \leq \int ^b_a f \leq U(P , f) \leq M\, (b - a)\] for any partition \(P\). □

Theorem 5.2.9 (Mean Value Theorem of Integrals). Suppose that \(f\) is continuous and bounded in the closed and bounded interval \([a,b]\), then there exists a number \(c\) with \(a < c < b\) such that \[\int ^b_a f(x)\,dx = (b-a)\, f(c)\]

Proof. From the above Remark; if \(M\) and \(m\) are bounds for \(f\), then \[m\, (b -a )\leq \int ^b_af(x)\, dx \leq M\, (b - a)\]

\[\implies \quad m\leq \dfrac {1}{(b - a)}\, \int ^b_af(x)\, dx \leq M\] This shows that \(\frac {1}{b - a}\,\int ^b_a f(x)\, dx\) is a value between \(m\) and \(M\) of a continuous function on \([a,b]\). Therefore, by the intermediate value theorem for continuous functions on \([a,b]\, f\) takes this value at some point \(c\in c[a,b]\). Thus \(\frac {1}{b - a}\, \int ^b_a f(x) \,dx = f(c)\) for some \(c\) in \([a,b]\). Hence \[\int ^b_af(x)\, dx = (b - a)\, f(c)\] □

Theorem 5.2.10 (Generalized Mean Value Theorem for Integrals). Suppose that \(f,\, g\) are continuous on \([a,b]\) and \(g(x)\geq 0\) for all \(x\in [a,b]\), then there exist a point \(c\in (a,b)\) such that \[\int ^b_af(x)\, g(x) \, dx = f(c)\, \int ^b_ag(x)\, dx\]

Proof. Let \(m\) and \(M\) be bounds of \(f\) in \([a,b]\), then \(m\leq f(x) \leq M\) for all \(x\in [a,b]\). Since \(g(x)\geq 0\) for all \(x\in [a,b]\), we have \(m\, g(x) \leq f(x)\, g(x) \leq M\, g(x)\) for all \(x\in [a,b]\). This implies that \[m\, \int ^b_ag(x)\, dx \leq \int ^b_af(x)\, g(x)\, dx \leq M\, \int ^b_ag(x)\,dx\] Thus, there is a number \(\mu \) with \(m\leq \mu \leq M\) such that \[\int ^b_af(x)\, g(x)\, dx = \mu \, \int ^b_ag(x)\, dx\] But since \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\), by the Intermediate Value Theorem there exists \(c\in [a,b]\) such that \(f(c) = \mu \). This implies that \[\int ^b_af(x)\, g(x)\, dx = f(c)\, \int ^b_ag(x)\,dx\qquad \text {as required}\] □

Example 5.2.11. Find a point \(c\) in \([0,1]\) such that \(\int ^1_0 \frac {x}{1 + x^2}\,dx = 1\)

Solution. Let \(f(x) = x\) and \(g(x) = \frac {1}{1 + x^2}\). Then \(f\) and \(g\) satisfy the condition of the Generalized Mean Value Theorem. Therefore, there exists a point \(c\in [0,1]\) such that \begin {align*} \int ^1_0x\,\left (\dfrac {1}{1 + x^2}\right )\, dx & = f(c)\, \int ^1_0\dfrac {1}{1 + x^2}\, dx\\ & = c\, \int ^1_0\dfrac {1}{1 + x^2}\, dx \end {align*}

But \(\int ^1_0\frac {1}{1 + x^2}\, dx = \left .\tan ^{-1}(x)\right |^1_0 = \tan ^{-1}(1) - \tan ^{-1}(0) = \frac {\pi }{4} - 0 = \frac {\pi }{4}\) and also

\(\int ^1_0\frac {x}{1 + x^2}\, dx = \left .\frac {1}{2}\, \ln (1 + x^2)\right |^1_0 = \frac {1}{2}\, \ln (2)\)

Thus \(\frac {1}{2}\, \ln (2) = \frac {\pi }{4}\, c\implies c = \frac {2}{\pi }\, \ln (2)\)
