4.7 Practice Problems

Problem 4.7.1. Show that a function of bounded variation on \([a,b]\) is bounded, and give an example of a continuous function that is not of bounded variation.

Show solution

Solution. Let \(V\) be the total variation of \(f\) on \([a,b]\). For any \(x\in [a,b]\) the partition \(\{a,x,b\}\) gives \[|f(x)-f(a)| \leq |f(x)-f(a)| + |f(b)-f(x)| \leq V,\] so \(|f(x)| \leq |f(a)| + V\) for every \(x\), and \(f\) is bounded.

For the example take \[f(x) = \begin {cases} x\sin \left (\frac {\pi }{x}\right ), & 0<x\leq 1,\\ 0, & x=0,\end {cases}\] which is continuous on \([0,1]\), including at \(0\) since \(|f(x)|\leq x\). At the points \(x_k = \frac {2}{2k+1}\) the function takes the values \(\pm x_k\) alternately, so a partition through those points contributes variation at least \(\sum _k \frac {2}{2k+1}\), which diverges. Hence the total variation is infinite.

Continuity does not imply bounded variation, and bounded variation does not imply continuity — a step function has variation equal to its jump. The two conditions are independent, which is why the class of functions of bounded variation is introduced separately.
