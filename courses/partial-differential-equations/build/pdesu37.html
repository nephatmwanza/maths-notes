6.5 Practice Problems

Problem 6.5.1. Show that \(u(x, t) = (1 + 4\kappa t)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/(1 + 4\kappa t)}\) solves \(u_t = \kappa u_{xx}\) with \(u(x, 0) = e^{-x^2}\), and interpret it with the heat kernel.

Show solution

Solution. Write \(s = 1 + 4\kappa t\). Then \(u_t = 4\kappa \bigl (-\frac 1{2s} + \frac {x^2}{s^2}\bigr )u\), and \(u_x = -\frac {2x}su\), \(u_{xx} = \bigl (-\frac 2s + \frac {4x^2}{s^2}\bigr )u\), so \(u_t = \kappa u_{xx}\). The data \(e^{-x^2}\) are \(\sqrt \pi \) times a normal density of variance \(\frac 12\); convolving with \(\Phi \), of variance \(2\kappa t\), gives \(\sqrt \pi \) times a normal density of variance \(\frac 12 + 2\kappa t = s/2\), which is \(u\).

Problem 6.5.2. Let \(u_t = u_{xx}\) on \(0 < x < 1\) with \(u(0, t) = u(1, t) = 0\) and \(u(x, 0) = x(1 - x)\). Without solving, show that \(0 \leq u(x, t) \leq \pi ^{-1}e^{-\pi ^2t}\sin \pi x\).

Show solution

Solution. The lower bound is the minimum principle, since \(u \geq 0\) on the parabolic boundary. For the upper bound, \(v = \pi ^{-1}e^{-\pi ^2t}\sin \pi x\) solves the heat equation and vanishes at the ends, so by comparison it suffices that \(x(1 - x) \leq \pi ^{-1}\sin \pi x\) on \([0, 1]\). Both sides are symmetric about \(\frac 12\); the difference \(d(x) = \pi ^{-1}\sin \pi x - x(1 - x)\) has \(d(0) = 0\) and \(d'(x) = \cos \pi x - (1 - 2x) \geq 0\) on \([0, \frac 12]\), because the concave function \(\cos \pi x\) lies above its chord \(1 - 2x\) there. So \(d \geq 0\).

Problem 6.5.3. Prove by the energy method that the Neumann problem \(u_t = \kappa \Delta u\) in \(\Omega \), \(\partial _\nu u = 0\) on \(\partial \Omega \), \(u(x, 0) = g\), has at most one solution, and that \(\int _\Omega u\,dx\) is constant. Interpret.

Show solution

Solution. For the difference \(w\) of two solutions, \(\frac {d}{dt}\int w^2 = 2\kappa \int w\Delta w = 2\kappa \int _{\partial \Omega }w\,\partial _\nu w\,dS - 2\kappa \int |\nabla w|^2 = -2\kappa \int |\nabla w|^2 \leq 0\), and \(\int w^2 = 0\) at \(t = 0\), so \(w = 0\). Also \(\frac {d}{dt}\int u = \kappa \int \Delta u = \kappa \int _{\partial \Omega }\partial _\nu u\,dS = 0\): an insulated body keeps its heat, and tends to the uniform temperature equal to its initial mean.

Problem 6.5.4. Find the depth at which the daily temperature wave is reduced to \(10\%\), for \(\kappa = 5 \times 10^{-7}\) m\(^2\)/s, and the time lag of the daily maximum there.

Show solution

Solution. \(q = \sqrt {\omega /2\kappa }\) with \(\omega = 2\pi /86400 = 7.27 \times 10^{-5}\) s\(^{-1}\), so \(q = \sqrt {72.7} = 8.53\) m\(^{-1}\) and the depth is \(\ln 10/q = 0.27\) m. The phase lag there is \(qx = \ln 10 = 2.30\) radians, a time lag of \(2.30/\omega = 31\,700\) s, about \(8.8\) hours: the afternoon maximum arrives at \(27\) cm around midnight.

Problem 6.5.5. Using the diffusion length, estimate how long heat takes to diffuse through \(1\) m of steel, with \(\kappa = 1.2 \times 10^{-5}\) m\(^2\)/s, and through \(1\) cm.

Show solution

Solution. Setting \(\sqrt {4\kappa t} = L\) gives \(t = L^2/4\kappa \): for \(1\) m, \(t = 1/(4.8 \times 10^{-5}) = 20\,800\) s, about \(6\) hours; for \(1\) cm, \(10^{-4}\) of that, about \(2\) seconds. The quadratic dependence on \(L\) is why a thin pan heats quickly and a thick casting slowly.

Problem 6.5.6. Show that if \(u\) solves \(u_t = \kappa u_{xx}\) then so do \(u(\lambda x, \lambda ^2t)\), \(u_x\), and \(xu_x + 2tu_t\). Hence show that \(x\,t^{-3/2}e^{-x^2/4\kappa t}\) is a solution, and interpret it.

Show solution

Solution. The first is the scaling invariance; the second follows by differentiating the equation; the third is \(\frac {d}{d\lambda }u(\lambda x, \lambda ^2t)\) at \(\lambda = 1\), a limit of solutions. Applying the second to \(\Phi \) gives \(\Phi _x = -\frac {x}{2\kappa t}\Phi \), a constant multiple of \(xt^{-3/2}e^{-x^2/4\kappa t}\). It is the solution on the half-line \(x > 0\) with \(u(0, t) = 0\) produced by a dipole, a source and an equal sink at the origin, and it is the derivative of the kernel along the direction of the dipole.
