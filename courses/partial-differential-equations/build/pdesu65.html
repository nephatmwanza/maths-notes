11.7 Practice Problems

Problem 11.7.1. Solve Laplace’s equation in the annulus \(1 < r < 2\) with \(u = 0\) on \(r = 1\) and \(u = \cos \theta \) on \(r = 2\).

Show solution

Solution. Try \(u = (Ar + B/r)\cos \theta \), harmonic since \(r^{\pm 1}\cos \theta \) are. Then \(A + B = 0\) and \(2A + B/2 = 1\), so \(A = \frac 23\), \(B = -\frac 23\), and \(u = \frac 23\bigl (r - \frac 1r\bigr )\cos \theta \).

Problem 11.7.2. Show that \(\int _0^1J_0(jr)\,r\,dr = J_1(j)/j\), and derive the coefficients \(c_n\) for the cooling cylinder.

Show solution

Solution. The recurrence \((x^mJ_m)' = x^mJ_{m - 1}\) with \(m = 1\) gives \((xJ_1(x))' = xJ_0(x)\), so \(\int _0^1J_0(jr)\,r\,dr = j^{-2}\int _0^jxJ_0(x)\,dx = j^{-2}\cdot jJ_1(j)\). Dividing by the norm \(\frac 12J_1(j)^2\) gives \(c_n = 2/(j_{0n}J_1(j_{0n}))\).

Problem 11.7.3. The surface of the unit sphere is held at potential \(\cos \theta \). Find the potential inside and outside, the latter decaying at infinity.

Show solution

Solution. \(\cos \theta = P_1(\cos \theta )\). Inside, \(u = rP_1 = r\cos \theta = z\), a uniform field. Outside, \(u = r^{-2}\cos \theta \), the field of a dipole. Both equal \(\cos \theta \) at \(r = 1\).

Problem 11.7.4. A ball of unit radius with \(\kappa = 1\), initially at temperature \(1\), has its surface held at \(0\). Find the temperature at the centre.

Show solution

Solution. For radial \(u\), \(\Delta u = \frac 1r(ru)_{rr}\), so \(v = ru\) satisfies \(v_t = v_{rr}\) with \(v(0, t) = v(1, t) = 0\) and \(v(r, 0) = r\). The sine coefficients of \(r\) are \(2\int _0^1r\sin n\pi r\,dr = 2(-1)^{n + 1}/n\pi \), so \[ u = \sum _{n \geq 1}\frac {2(-1)^{n + 1}}{n\pi }e^{-n^2\pi ^2t}\,\frac {\sin n\pi r}r , \] and since \(\sin (n\pi r)/r \to n\pi \), the centre temperature is \(u(0, t) = 2\sum _{n \geq 1}(-1)^{n + 1}e^{-n^2\pi ^2t}\).

Problem 11.7.5. Which modes of a circular drum are excited by striking it exactly at the centre, and why does a drummer strike off centre?

Show solution

Solution. A blow at the centre is symmetric about it, so it excites only the radially symmetric modes \((0, n)\), whose frequency ratios \(1, 2.295, 3.598, \ldots \) are far from harmonic and give a dull thud. Striking off centre excites the modes with \(m \geq 1\) as well; the \((1, 1)\), \((2, 1)\), \((3, 1)\) modes have ratios close to \(2 : 3 : 4\) once the air loading of a kettledrum shifts them, a harmonic series on a missing fundamental, which gives the timpani its pitch.

Problem 11.7.6. In Kelvin’s calculation, how does the estimated age change if the diffusivity is doubled, or if the measured gradient is halved?

Show solution

Solution. \(t = T_0^2/\pi \kappa G^2\) is inversely proportional to \(\kappa \) and to \(G^2\): doubling \(\kappa \) halves the age to \(48\) million years, and halving \(G\) multiplies it by \(4\), to \(384\) million years. The estimate is very sensitive to the gradient, which is one reason for Kelvin’s wide range.
