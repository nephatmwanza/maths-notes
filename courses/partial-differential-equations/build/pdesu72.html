12.7 Practice Problems

Problem 12.7.1. Show that \(E[w] = \int _\Omega (\frac 12|\nabla w|^2 - fw)\) is bounded below on functions vanishing on \(\partial \Omega \).

Show solution

Solution. By Cauchy–Schwarz and Poincaré with \(C = d/\pi \), \(\int fw \leq \|f\|\,\|w\| \leq C\|f\|\,\|w\|_E\), so \(E[w] \geq \frac 12\|w\|_E^2 - C\|f\|\,\|w\|_E \geq -\frac 12C^2\|f\|^2\), the minimum of the quadratic \(\frac 12s^2 - C\|f\|s\).

Problem 12.7.2. Find the solution of \(-u'' = 1\) on \((0, 1)\) with zero boundary values, its energy, and the energy of the trial function \(w = \frac 12\sin \pi x\).

Show solution

Solution. \(u = \frac 12x(1 - x)\), with \(\int u'^2 = \int (\frac 12 - x)^2 = \frac 1{12}\) and \(\int u = \frac 1{12}\), so \(E[u] = \frac 1{24} - \frac 1{12} = -\frac 1{24} = -0.04167\). For \(w\): \(\int w'^2 = \frac {\pi ^2}4\cdot \frac 12 = \frac {\pi ^2}8\) and \(\int w = \frac 1\pi \), so \(E[w] = \frac {\pi ^2}{16} - \frac 1\pi = 0.6169 - 0.3183 = 0.2986\), far above, because the amplitude is wrong. The best multiple \(c\sin \pi x\) minimises \(\frac {\pi ^2}4c^2 - \frac {2c}\pi \), at \(c = 4/\pi ^3\), with energy \(-4/\pi ^4 = -0.04106\), just above \(-\frac 1{24}\).

Problem 12.7.3. Write the weak form of \(-(a(x)u')' = f\) on \((0, 1)\), \(u(0) = u(1) = 0\), where \(a_0 \leq a(x) \leq a_1\) with \(a_0 > 0\), and show that the bilinear form is coercive.

Show solution

Solution. Multiply by \(v\) vanishing at the ends and integrate by parts: \(\int _0^1au'v'\,dx = \int _0^1fv\,dx\) for all \(v \in H^1_0(0, 1)\). The form \(a(u, v) = \int au'v'\) is symmetric with \(a(v, v) \geq a_0\int v'^2 = a_0\|v\|_E^2\), so it is coercive with \(\alpha = a_0\), and bounded by \(a_1\). Lax–Milgram (or Riesz with this inner product) gives a unique weak solution, even when \(a\) is discontinuous, as for a rod made of two materials.

Problem 12.7.4. Use the trial function \(1 - r^2\) to bound the lowest frequency of a circular drum of unit radius, and compare with the exact \(\lambda _1 = j_{01}^2\).

Show solution

Solution. \(\int |\nabla w|^2 = \int _0^14r^2\cdot 2\pi r\,dr = 2\pi \) and \(\int w^2 = 2\pi \int _0^1(1 - r^2)^2r\,dr = \frac \pi 3\), so \(R = 6\), against \(j_{01}^2 = 5.783\): an upper bound \(3.7\%\) high, and for the frequency \(\sqrt R\), only \(1.9\%\).

Problem 12.7.5. Show that for the Neumann problem \(-\Delta u = f\), \(\partial _\nu u = 0\), the weak form forces \(\int _\Omega f = 0\), and explain why uniqueness holds only up to a constant.

Show solution

Solution. The weak form is \(\int \nabla u\cdot \nabla v = \int fv\) for all \(v \in H^1(\Omega )\), with no boundary condition on \(v\) (the Neumann condition is natural: it comes out of the integration by parts). Taking \(v = 1\) gives \(0 = \int f\). If \(u\) is a solution so is \(u + c\), since \(\nabla c = 0\); on the subspace of functions with mean zero the form is coercive by the Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality, and the solution is unique there.

Problem 12.7.6. Using the energy method, show that the solution of \(u_t = \kappa u_{xx}\) on \((0, L)\) with \(u = 0\) at the ends satisfies \(\int u_x^2\) non-increasing, and deduce a bound on \(\int u_x(x, t)^2\) in terms of the initial data.

Show solution

Solution. \(\frac {d}{dt}\int u_x^2 = 2\int u_xu_{xt} = -2\int u_{xx}u_t = -2\kappa \int u_{xx}^2 \leq 0\), integrating by parts with \(u_t = 0\) at the ends. Hence \(\int u_x(x, t)^2 \leq \int g'^2\). Integrating the identity \(\frac {d}{dt}\int u^2 = -2\kappa \int u_x^2\) from \(0\) to \(t\) and using the monotonicity gives the smoothing estimate \(2\kappa t\int u_x(x, t)^2 \leq \int g^2\): the gradient becomes finite at once, even for rough data.
