1.8 Practice Problems

Problem 1.8.1. Solve \(y' + y\tan t = \sec t\), \(y(0) = 2\), on \((-\pi /2, \pi /2)\), and explain why the solution cannot be continued past \(\pm \pi /2\) by the theorem.

Show solution

Solution. Here \(p = \tan t\), \(P(t) = \int _0^t\tan s\,ds = -\log \cos t\), and \(e^{P} = \sec t\). By Theorem 1.2.1, \(y = \cos t\,\bigl (2 + \int _0^t\sec ^2s\,ds\bigr ) = \cos t(2 + \tan t) = 2\cos t + \sin t\). The theorem guarantees the solution on the largest interval containing \(0\) on which \(\tan t\) and \(\sec t\) are continuous, which is \((-\pi /2, \pi /2)\). The formula \(2\cos t + \sin t\) happens to extend smoothly beyond, but the equation itself is undefined at \(\pm \pi /2\), so the theorem says nothing there.

Problem 1.8.2. Solve \(y' = t y^2\), \(y(0) = y_0\), and find the interval of existence. Compare \(y_0 = 1\) and \(y_0 = -1\).

Show solution

Solution. For \(y_0 \neq 0\), separation gives \(-1/y = t^2/2 - 1/y_0\), so \(y = 2y_0/(2 - y_0t^2)\); for \(y_0 = 0\), \(y \equiv 0\). With \(y_0 = 1\), \(y = 2/(2 - t^2)\), which exists only on \((-\sqrt 2, \sqrt 2)\) and tends to \(+\infty \) at the ends: the solution blows up in finite time although \(f(t, y) = ty^2\) is smooth everywhere. With \(y_0 = -1\), \(y = -2/(2 + t^2)\), which exists for all \(t\) and tends to \(0\). The interval of existence of a non-linear equation depends on the initial value, unlike the linear case.

Problem 1.8.3. Show that \((3x^2 + y\cos x)\,dx + (\sin x - 4y^3)\,dy = 0\) is exact and find its solutions.

Show solution

Solution. \(M_y = \cos x = N_x\). Integrating \(M\) in \(x\): \(F = x^3 + y\sin x + \psi (y)\), and \(F_y = \sin x + \psi ' = \sin x - 4y^3\) gives \(\psi = -y^4\). The solutions satisfy \(x^3 + y\sin x - y^4 = C\).

Problem 1.8.4. Find an integrating factor for \((x^2 + y^2 + x)\,dx + xy\,dy = 0\) and solve the equation.

Show solution

Solution. \(M_y = 2y\), \(N_x = y\), and \((M_y - N_x)/N = y/(xy) = 1/x\), a function of \(x\) alone, so \(\mu = x\) by Theorem 1.4.3. Then \((x^3 + xy^2 + x^2)\,dx + x^2y\,dy = 0\) is exact, with \(F = \frac 14x^4 + \frac 12x^2y^2 + \frac 13x^3\), and the solutions satisfy \(3x^4 + 6x^2y^2 + 4x^3 = C\).

Problem 1.8.5. The Riccati equation \(y' = 1 + t^2 - y^2\) has the solution \(y_1 = t\). Find the general solution.

Show solution

Solution. Here \(q_0 = 1 + t^2\), \(q_1 = 0\), \(q_2 = -1\). By Theorem 1.5.2(i), \(y = t + 1/v\) with \(v' = -(0 + 2(-1)t)v - (-1) = 2tv + 1\). The integrating factor is \(e^{-t^2}\), so \(v = e^{t^2}\bigl (C + \int _0^te^{-s^2}\,ds\bigr )\) and \[ y = t + \frac {e^{-t^2}}{C + \int _0^te^{-s^2}\,ds} . \] The integral is \(\frac {\sqrt \pi }{2}\operatorname {erf}(t)\), a non-elementary function, as Theorem 1.5.2(ii) leads one to expect.

Problem 1.8.6. For \(y' = y^2(1 - y)\), draw the phase line, classify the equilibria, and describe the solutions with \(y(0) = -0.1\), \(0.1\) and \(1.5\).

Show solution

Solution. The equilibria are \(0\) and \(1\). The sign of \(f\) is positive for \(y < 0\) and for \(0 < y < 1\), and negative for \(y > 1\). So \(1\) is asymptotically stable (\(f'(1) = -1\)), and \(0\) is semi-stable (\(f'(0) = 0\)): solutions approach it from below and leave it above. Starting at \(-0.1\), the solution increases towards \(0\); at \(0.1\) it increases towards \(1\); at \(1.5\) it decreases towards \(1\). The first two show that a solution starting near \(0\) on one side tends to \(0\) and on the other side goes away from it.

Problem 1.8.7. Modify Example 1.6.2 so that the harvest is proportional to the stock, \(y' = y(1 - y) - Ey\), with effort \(E \geq 0\). Find the equilibria and their stability, the sustainable yield \(Ey^*\) at the stable equilibrium, and the effort that maximises it. Why is proportional harvesting safer than a fixed quota?

Show solution

Solution. The equilibria are \(0\) and \(y^* = 1 - E\) (for \(E < 1\)). With \(f'(y) = 1 - E - 2y\), \(f'(0) = 1 - E > 0\) and \(f'(1 - E) = -(1 - E) < 0\), so \(y^*\) is stable and \(0\) unstable. The yield is \(E(1 - E)\), maximised at \(E = \frac 12\) with yield \(\frac 14\), the same maximum as before. But now, for every \(E < 1\), any positive stock recovers to \(y^* > 0\): there is no cliff, because the catch falls automatically when the stock falls. Here the equilibria \(0\) and \(1 - E\) exchange stability at \(E = 1\) instead of disappearing (a transcritical bifurcation), and a fixed quota \(h\) near \(\frac 14\) is dangerous because a small error or shock pushes the stock below the unstable \(y_-\).
