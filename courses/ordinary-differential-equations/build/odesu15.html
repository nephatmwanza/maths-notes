2.7 Practice Problems

Problem 2.7.1. Compute the Picard iterates for \(x' = x\), \(x(0) = 1\), and show that \(\phi _k(t) = \sum _{j=0}^kt^j/j!\). What does Theorem 2.3.1 give as the interval of existence for \(R\) with \(a\) and \(b\) arbitrary, and how does this compare with the truth?

Show solution

Solution. By induction, if \(\phi _k = \sum _{j \leq k}t^j/j!\) then \(\phi _{k+1} = 1 + \int _0^t\phi _k = 1 + \sum _{j \leq k}t^{j+1}/(j + 1)!\), the next partial sum. On \(R\), \(M = 1 + b\) (the maximum of \(|x|\) for \(|x - 1| \leq b\)), so \(\alpha = \min (a, b/(1 + b))\), which is less than \(1\) for every \(b\). The theorem guarantees existence only on \(|t| < 1\), although the solution \(e^t\) exists for all \(t\); global existence for linear equations is proved in the next section.

Problem 2.7.2. For \(x' = t^2 + x^2\), \(x(0) = 0\), on \(R = \{|t| \leq 1, |x| \leq 1\}\), find \(M\), \(L\) and the interval of existence given by Theorem 2.3.1. How many Picard iterates guarantee an error below \(10^{-6}\) on that interval?

Show solution

Solution. \(M = \max (t^2 + x^2) = 2\), and \(L = \max |\partial f/\partial x| = \max |2x| = 2\). Then \(\alpha = \min (1, 1/2) = \frac 12\). On \(|t| \leq \frac 12\), \(L|t| \leq 1\), and the error bound is \(\frac ML\frac {1}{(k + 1)!}e = \frac {e}{(k + 1)!}\). Since \(9! = 362\,880\) gives \(7.5 \times 10^{-6}\) and \(10! = 3\,628\,800\) gives \(7.5 \times 10^{-7}\), \(k + 1 = 10\), that is \(k = 9\) iterates, suffice.

Problem 2.7.3. Show that \(x' = \sqrt {|x|}\), \(x(0) = 0\), has infinitely many solutions, and that \(x(0) = 1\) has a unique solution near \(t = 0\). Reconcile the two with Theorem 2.3.1.

Show solution

Solution. \(x \equiv 0\) is a solution, and so is \(x(t) = 0\) for \(t \leq c\), \((t - c)^2/4\) for \(t > c\), for every \(c \geq 0\): on \(t > c\), \(x' = (t - c)/2 = \sqrt {x}\). Near \(x = 1\), \(\sqrt {|x|}\) is continuously differentiable, so it is Lipschitz on a rectangle around \((0, 1)\) (Lemma 2.2.2) and the theorem applies. The failure at \(x = 0\) comes from \(\frac {d}{dx}\sqrt x = \frac {1}{2\sqrt x}\) being unbounded there.

Problem 2.7.4. Let \(A(t)\) be a continuous \(n \times n\) matrix function on \([t_0, t_1]\) and \(x\) a solution of \(x' = A(t)x\). Use Gronwall’s inequality to show that \(\|x(t)\| \leq \|x(t_0)\|\exp \int _{t_0}^t\|A(s)\|\,ds\).

Show solution

Solution. \(x(t) = x(t_0) + \int _{t_0}^tA(s)x(s)\,ds\), so \(u = \|x\|\) satisfies \(u(t) \leq \|x(t_0)\| + \int _{t_0}^t\|A(s)\|u(s)\,ds\). The general form of Theorem 2.4.1, with \(c \equiv \|x(t_0)\|\) and \(k = \|A\|\), gives the bound. In particular a solution of a linear system cannot blow up on a bounded interval, which is the key to global existence in the next section.

Problem 2.7.5. The equation \(x' = \sin x\) is solved twice from initial values \(1\) and \(1.01\). Bound the difference of the solutions on \([0, 2]\). A computer solves the equation with an error of at most \(10^{-8}\) in the right-hand side at every step; bound the resulting error at \(t = 2\).

Show solution

Solution. \(L = 1\). By Theorem 2.4.2 with \(\varepsilon = 0\), \(|x(t) - y(t)| \leq 0.01e^t \leq 0.01e^2 = 0.074\) on \([0, 2]\). With equal initial values and \(\varepsilon = 10^{-8}\), the error at \(t = 2\) is at most \(10^{-8}(e^2 - 1) = 6.4 \times 10^{-8}\).

Problem 2.7.6. For \(y' = 3y^{2/3}\), \(y(0) = 0\), compute the Picard iterates starting from \(\phi _0 = 0\) and from \(\phi _0 = t^3\). Explain why Banach’s theorem does not apply.

Show solution

Solution. From \(\phi _0 = 0\), every iterate is \(0\). From \(\phi _0 = t^3\), \(\phi _1 = \int _0^t3(s^3)^{2/3}\,ds = \int _0^t3s^2\,ds = t^3\), so \(t^3\) is also a fixed point. A contraction has only one fixed point, so the integral operator is not a contraction in any metric of the kind used; the reason is the failure of the Lipschitz condition at \(y = 0\) (Example 2.2.3(c)).

Problem 2.7.7. With \(\|\phi \|_\ast = \sup _{t \in J}e^{-2L|t - t_0|}\|\phi (t)\|\), show that the integral operator \(T\) of Section 2.1 satisfies \(\|T\phi - T\psi \|_\ast \leq \frac 12\|\phi - \psi \|_\ast \) under the Lipschitz condition.

Show solution

Solution. For \(t \geq t_0\), \begin {align*} \|T\phi (t) - T\psi (t)\| &\leq L\int _{t_0}^te^{2L(s - t_0)}e^{-2L(s - t_0)}\|\phi (s) - \psi (s)\|\,ds\\ &\leq L\|\phi - \psi \|_\ast \int _{t_0}^te^{2L(s - t_0)}\,ds \leq \frac 12\|\phi - \psi \|_\ast e^{2L(t - t_0)} . \end {align*}

Multiplying by \(e^{-2L(t - t_0)}\) and taking the supremum gives the result; \(t < t_0\) is the same. Since \(\|\cdot \|_\ast \) lies between \(e^{-2L\alpha }\|\cdot \|_\infty \) and \(\|\cdot \|_\infty \), the space is still complete, and Banach’s theorem gives a unique fixed point on the whole interval \(J\), with no restriction \(L\alpha < 1\).
