4 Linear Systems

Linear systems are the one large class of differential equations with a complete general theory. They describe small oscillations, electrical networks, compartment models in pharmacology, and every linearisation of a non-linear system about a solution, which is how they enter the stability theory later in the course. This section treats the system \[ x' = A(t)x + g(t), \qquad x \in \mathbb R^n, \] with \(A\) and \(g\) continuous on an open interval \(I\). By Theorem 3.2.1, every initial value problem has a unique solution on the whole of \(I\), so questions of existence are settled; what remains is the structure of the solutions. The next section specialises to constant \(A\), where the solutions can be written down.
