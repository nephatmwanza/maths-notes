8.6 Practice problems

Problem 8.6.1. A process with three factors was run as a single replicate of a \(2^3\) design. In standard order the responses were \(10\), \(16\), \(18\), \(26\), \(22\), \(16\), \(32\), \(20\).

(a)
Estimate the interaction effects \(AB\), \(AC\) and \(ABC\). (The other estimates are \(A = -1\), \(B = 8\), \(C = 5\), \(BC = -1\).)
(b)
Plot all the effects on normal probability paper and identify those that appear significant.
(c)
Prepare an analysis of variance table based on the effects identified in (b), keeping any main effect needed for hierarchy, with the remaining effects as error, and test the significance of the effects.
(d)
Write down the regression equation for the significant effects.

Show solution

Solution. (a) Using the table of signs, \begin {align*} \text {Contrast}_{AB} &= 10 - 16 - 18 + 26 + 22 - 16 - 32 + 20 = -4 , \\ \text {Contrast}_{AC} &= 10 - 16 + 18 - 26 - 22 + 16 - 32 + 20 = -32 , \\ \text {Contrast}_{ABC} &= -10 + 16 + 18 - 26 + 22 - 16 - 32 + 20 = -8 , \end {align*}

and dividing by \(4\): \(AB = -1\), \(AC = -8\), \(ABC = -2\).

(b) The ordered effects \(-8\) (\(AC\)), \(-2\) (\(ABC\)), \(-1\), \(-1\), \(-1\) (\(A\), \(AB\), \(BC\)), \(5\) (\(C\)), \(8\) (\(B\)) are plotted against the quantiles \(-1.47\), \(-0.79\), \(-0.37\), \(0\), \(0.37\), \(0.79\), \(1.47\). The four effects near zero lie on a line through the origin; \(B\), \(C\) and \(AC\) fall well off it.

(c) Each sum of squares is \(2\hat E^2\): \(A\): \(2\), \(B\): \(128\), \(C\): \(50\), \(AC\): \(128\). Keeping \(A\) because \(AC\) is in the model, the error is \(AB + BC + ABC = 2 + 2 + 8 = 12\) on \(3\) degrees of freedom, \(MSE = 4\), and \(SST = 320\).

source SS df \(F_0\) \(p\)
\(A\) \(2\) \(1\) \(0.5\) \(0.53\)
\(B\) \(128\) \(1\) \(32.0\) \(0.011\)
\(C\) \(50\) \(1\) \(12.5\) \(0.038\)
\(AC\) \(128\) \(1\) \(32.0\) \(0.011\)
error \(12\) \(3\)
total \(320\) \(7\)

With \(F_{0.05,1,3} = 10.13\), \(B\), \(C\) and \(AC\) are significant.

(d) The mean is \(160/8 = 20\) and each coefficient is half the effect: \[ \hat y = 20 - 0.5x_1 + 4x_2 + 2.5x_3 - 4x_1x_3 . \] The strong negative \(AC\) interaction means that \(C\) helps at the low level of \(A\) (by \(2.5 + 4 = 6.5\) per unit of \(x_3\)) but not at the high level (\(2.5 - 4 = -1.5\)).

Problem 8.6.2. The output \(y\) of a process was studied in a single replicate of a \(2^3\) design in factors \(A\), \(B\), \(C\). In standard order the responses were \(22\), \(22\), \(26\), \(57\), \(41\), \(19\), \(45\), \(44\).

(a)
Compute the estimates of all the main effects and interactions.
(b)
Plot the effects on a normal probability plot. Which effects appear significant?
(c)
Compute an analysis of variance table, assuming the effects \(A\) and \(ABC\) are negligible.
(d)
Compute a \(95\%\) confidence interval for the \(BC\) interaction effect.
(e)
Write down a regression model based on the significant effects in (b).
(f)
Estimate the mean output when \(A\) and \(B\) are at the low level and \(C\) at the high level.

Show solution

Solution. (a) The contrasts are \(A\): \(8\), \(B\): \(68\), \(C\): \(22\), \(AB\): \(52\), \(AC\): \(-54\), \(BC\): \(-10\), \(ABC\): \(-10\), and dividing by \(4\) the effects are \begin {gather*} A = 2 , \quad B = 17 , \quad C = 5.5 , \quad AB = 13 , \\ AC = -13.5 , \quad BC = -2.5 , \quad ABC = -2.5 . \end {gather*}

(b) Ordered: \(-13.5\) (\(AC\)), \(-2.5\), \(-2.5\) (\(BC\), \(ABC\)), \(2\) (\(A\)), \(5.5\) (\(C\)), \(13\) (\(AB\)), \(17\) (\(B\)). The effects \(A\), \(BC\), \(ABC\) and perhaps \(C\) lie near a line through the origin; \(B\), \(AB\) and \(AC\) fall well off it and appear significant.

(c) Each sum of squares is \(2\hat E^2\), and \(SST = 1374\). With \(A\) (\(8\)) and \(ABC\) (\(12.5\)) as error, \(SSE = 20.5\) on \(2\) degrees of freedom and \(MSE = 10.25\):

source SS df \(F_0\) \(p\)
\(B\) \(578.0\) \(1\) \(56.39\) \(0.017\)
\(C\) \(60.5\) \(1\) \(5.90\) \(0.136\)
\(AB\) \(338.0\) \(1\) \(32.98\) \(0.029\)
\(AC\) \(364.5\) \(1\) \(35.56\) \(0.027\)
\(BC\) \(12.5\) \(1\) \(1.22\) \(0.385\)
error \(20.5\) \(2\)
total \(1374.0\) \(7\)

With \(F_{0.05,1,2} = 18.51\), \(B\), \(AB\) and \(AC\) are significant; \(C\) and \(BC\) are not.

(d) \(\Var (\hat E) = \sigma ^2/2^{3-2} = \sigma ^2/2\), estimated by \(10.25/2\), so \(\operatorname {se}(\widehat {BC}) = 2.264\). With \(t_{0.025,2} = 4.303\) the interval is \(-2.5 \pm 4.303(2.264) = -2.5 \pm 9.74\), that is \((-12.24,\ 7.24)\). It contains zero, consistent with (c).

(e) Keeping \(A\) and \(C\) for hierarchy, since they appear in the significant interactions, and using half-effects with \(\bar y = 34.5\), \[ \hat y = 34.5 + 1.0x_1 + 8.5x_2 + 2.75x_3 + 6.5x_1x_2 - 6.75x_1x_3 . \]

(f) At \(x_1 = -1\), \(x_2 = -1\), \(x_3 = +1\), \[ \hat y = 34.5 - 1.0 - 8.5 + 2.75 + 6.5 + 6.75 = 41.0 , \] which equals the observed value \(41\) at that combination.

Problem 8.6.3. In a \(2^k\) design with \(n\) replicates, show that the estimate of an effect has variance \(\sigma ^2/(n2^{k-2})\), and find the number of replicates of a \(2^3\) design needed for the standard error of an effect to be at most \(0.5\sigma \).

Show solution

Solution. An effect estimate is \(\bar y_+ - \bar y_-\), the difference of two independent means of \(N/2 = n2^{k-1}\) observations each, so its variance is \(2\sigma ^2/(n2^{k-1}) = \sigma ^2/(n2^{k-2})\). For \(k = 3\) the standard error is \(\sigma /\sqrt {2n}\), which is at most \(0.5\sigma \) when \(2n \ge 4\): \(n = 2\) replicates, \(16\) runs.

Problem 8.6.4. Show that in the table of signs of a \(2^k\) design the product of the columns for two effects is the column for the effect whose letters are those appearing in exactly one of the two, and use this to show that any two columns are orthogonal.

Show solution

Solution. The column of an effect is the product of the main-effect columns of its letters. Multiplying two such columns, each main-effect column that appears in both is multiplied by itself, giving a column of \(+1\)’s, so only the letters in exactly one of the two remain: for example \(ABC \cdot BCD = AB^2C^2D = AD\). If the two effects differ, at least one letter remains, and the product is the column of an effect, which has equal numbers of \(+\) and \(-\) signs because each main-effect column does and the levels of different factors are crossed. So the inner product of the two columns, the sum of the product column, is zero.
