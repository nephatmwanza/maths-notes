2.7 Practice problems

Problem 2.7.1. Two methods of making concrete blocks were compared, using independent random samples of blocks. Method \(1\) gave \(n_1 = 12\) blocks with \(\bar y_1 = 48.2\) and \(s_1 = 3.1\); method \(2\) gave \(n_2 = 10\) blocks with \(\bar y_2 = 45.1\) and \(s_2 = 2.6\). Test whether the mean compressive strengths differ at \(\alpha = 0.05\), and give a \(95\%\) confidence interval for \(\mu _1 - \mu _2\).

Show solution

Solution. The pooled variance is \[ s_p^2 = \frac {11(3.1)^2 + 9(2.6)^2}{20} = \frac {105.71 + 60.84}{20} = 8.3275 , \qquad s_p = 2.886 , \] and the standard error is \(2.886\sqrt {1/12 + 1/10} = 1.2356\). Then \(t_0 = (48.2 - 45.1)/1.2356 = 2.509\). Since \(t_{0.025,20} = 2.086\), \(H_0\) is rejected (\(p = 0.021\)). The \(95\%\) interval is \(3.1 \pm 2.086(1.2356)\), that is \((0.52,\ 5.68)\).

Problem 2.7.2. Show that \(S_p^2\) is an unbiased estimator of \(\sigma ^2\), and that when \(n_1 = n_2\) the Welch statistic \(t_0^*\) equals the pooled statistic \(t_0\).

Show solution

Solution. \(E(S_i^2) = \sigma ^2\) for each sample, so \(E(S_p^2) = [(n_1 - 1)\sigma ^2 + (n_2 - 1)\sigma ^2]/(n_1 + n_2 - 2) = \sigma ^2\). If \(n_1 = n_2 = n\), then \(S_p^2 = (S_1^2 + S_2^2)/2\), and \[ S_p^2\Bigl (\frac 1n + \frac 1n\Bigr ) = \frac {S_1^2 + S_2^2}{n} = \frac {S_1^2}{n} + \frac {S_2^2}{n} , \] so the two denominators are equal and \(t_0^* = t_0\). Only the reference distribution differs: \(t_{2n-2}\) for the pooled test, \(t_\nu \) with \(\nu \le 2n - 2\) for Welch’s.

Problem 2.7.3. Show that Welch’s degrees of freedom satisfy \(\min (n_1, n_2) - 1 \le \nu \le n_1 + n_2 - 2\).

Show solution

Solution. Write \(u_i = s_i^2/n_i > 0\) and \(f_i = n_i - 1\), so \(\nu = (u_1 + u_2)^2/(u_1^2/f_1 + u_2^2/f_2)\). For the lower bound, with \(f = \min (f_1, f_2)\), \[ \frac {u_1^2}{f_1} + \frac {u_2^2}{f_2} \le \frac {u_1^2 + u_2^2}{f} \le \frac {(u_1 + u_2)^2}{f} , \] so \(\nu \ge f\). For the upper bound, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, \[ (u_1 + u_2)^2 = \Bigl (\frac {u_1}{\sqrt {f_1}}\sqrt {f_1} + \frac {u_2}{\sqrt {f_2}}\sqrt {f_2}\Bigr )^2 \le \Bigl (\frac {u_1^2}{f_1} + \frac {u_2^2}{f_2}\Bigr )(f_1 + f_2) , \] so \(\nu \le f_1 + f_2 = n_1 + n_2 - 2\), with equality when \(u_1/f_1 = u_2/f_2\).

Problem 2.7.4. An experimenter expects \(\sigma \approx 5\) and wants power \(0.80\) to detect a difference of \(4\) units between two treatment means, using a two-sided test at \(\alpha = 0.05\). Find the number of replicates per treatment from the normal approximation, and comment on the exact answer.

Show solution

Solution. With \(z_{0.025} = 1.960\) and \(z_{0.20} = 0.842\), \[ n = \frac {2(1.960 + 0.842)^2(25)}{16} = 24.53 , \] so \(25\) per treatment. The exact power from the non-central \(t\) distribution is \(0.791\) with \(n = 25\) and \(0.807\) with \(n = 26\), so \(26\) are required. As in the text, the approximation falls short by about one replicate, because it treats \(\sigma \) as known.

Problem 2.7.5. The systolic blood pressure of six patients was measured before and after a month on a new drug:

patient 1 2 3 4 5 6
before \(142\) \(150\) \(138\) \(161\) \(147\) \(155\)
after \(136\) \(144\) \(139\) \(150\) \(141\) \(149\)

Test whether the drug changes mean blood pressure, and give a \(95\%\) confidence interval for the mean reduction. What does this design not control?

Show solution

Solution. The reductions are \(d = (6, 6, -1, 11, 6, 6)\), with \(\bar d = 5.667\) and \(s_d = 3.830\). Then \[ t_0 = \frac {5.667}{3.830/\sqrt 6} = 3.624 , \] against \(t_{0.025,5} = 2.571\), so \(H_0\) is rejected (\(p = 0.015\)). The interval is \(5.667 \pm 2.571(1.5635)\), that is \((1.65,\ 9.69)\).

Each patient is a block, but the two “treatments” (before and after) cannot be randomised in order: after always follows before. Any change over the month, such as the season, the attention of being in a study, or regression towards the mean in patients selected for high pressure, is confounded with the drug. A control group taking a placebo, with patients allocated at random, would be needed to separate them.

Problem 2.7.6. In a paired design the two observations in a pair have variance \(\sigma _y^2\) and correlation \(\rho \). For what values of \(\rho \) does pairing give a smaller variance for the estimated difference than two independent samples of the same size? Explain why the comparison of the two tests also involves the degrees of freedom.

Show solution

Solution. The variances are \(2\sigma _y^2(1 - \rho )/n\) and \(2\sigma _y^2/n\), so pairing is better exactly when \(\rho > 0\); when units within a pair are negatively correlated it is worse. But the tests also differ in their error degrees of freedom, \(n - 1\) against \(2n - 2\), and so in the critical value \(t_{\alpha /2}\). For small \(n\) and small positive \(\rho \) the larger critical value of the paired test can outweigh its smaller variance. In practice pairs are formed because their members are alike, \(\rho \) is substantial, and the variance reduction dominates.
