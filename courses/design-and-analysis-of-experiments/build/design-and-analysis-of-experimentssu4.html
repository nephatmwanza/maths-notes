1.4 Blocking: the tyre example

Example 1.4.1. Suppose we want to compare two brands of tyres, \(A\) and \(B\), by the tread wear after a fixed distance. Possible complicating factors include

(i)
the type of car;
(ii)
road conditions;
(iii)
the type of driver;

and so on. How do we control for them? Known factors are handled by blocking, and unknown ones by randomisation. Consider the possible experimental strategies.

(a)
\(2n\) cars. Choose \(n\) cars at random for brand \(A\) and fit the other \(n\) with brand \(B\). Then \(\mu _A - \mu _B\) is estimated by \(\bar y_A - \bar y_B\), with \[ E(\bar Y_A - \bar Y_B) = \mu _A - \mu _B , \qquad \Var (\bar Y_A - \bar Y_B) = \frac {2\sigma ^2}{n} , \] where \(\sigma ^2\) is the variance of the wear on a single car. The only way to reduce this variance is to increase \(n\).
(b)
\(n\) good drivers and \(n\) rough drivers. There are now two possible strategies.
(i)
Randomly assign \(n\) of the \(2n\) drivers to brand \(A\) and the other \(n\) to brand \(B\), ignoring the type of driver. The variance \(\sigma _1^2\) of \(\bar y_A - \bar y_B\) then includes variation between brands and among drivers.
(ii)
Randomly assign half (\(n/2\)) of the good drivers to brand \(A\) and the other half to brand \(B\), and do the same for the rough drivers. Writing \(\bar y_{1A}\) for the mean of the good drivers on brand \(A\), and so on, estimate \(\mu _A - \mu _B\) by \[ \frac {\bar y_{1A} + \bar y_{2A} - (\bar y_{1B} + \bar y_{2B})}{2} , \] with variance \(\sigma _2^2\).

The second strategy gives the higher precision, \(\sigma _2^2 < \sigma _1^2\). Proposition 1.4.2 below says by how much.

(c)
\(n\) cars, both brands on each. Fit brand \(A\) on one side of each car and brand \(B\) on the other, choosing the side at random. The car is now a block of two units, and each car gives a difference \(d_k = y_{kA} - y_{kB}\) in which the car’s own effect cancels.

To compare the strategies we need a model for the wear. Write the wear of a tyre of brand \(T\) on car (or driver) \(k\) as \[ y = \mu _T + \beta _k + \varepsilon , \] where \(\beta _k\) is the effect of the car or driver and \(\varepsilon \), with variance \(\sigma ^2\), is the remaining variation of a tyre about its car’s level.

Proposition 1.4.2. In strategy (b), let good drivers have \(\beta = -\delta \) and rough drivers \(\beta = +\delta \), and let the errors be independent with variance \(\sigma ^2\). Then \[ \sigma _1^2 = \frac {2\sigma ^2}{n} + \frac {4\delta ^2}{2n - 1} , \qquad \sigma _2^2 = \frac {2\sigma ^2}{n} . \]

Proof. For (i), apply Theorem 1.3.2 conditionally on the errors, with unit values \(u_k = \beta _k + \varepsilon _k\). The driver effects are \(n\) values \(-\delta \) and \(n\) values \(+\delta \), with mean \(0\) and \(\sum \beta _k^2 = 2n\delta ^2\), so taking expectations over the errors, \[ E(S^2) = \sigma ^2 + \frac {2n\delta ^2}{2n - 1} , \qquad \text {and}\qquad \sigma _1^2 = \frac 2nE(S^2) = \frac {2\sigma ^2}{n} + \frac {4\delta ^2}{2n - 1} . \] For (ii), each of the four group means is over \(n/2\) drivers of the same type. In \(\bar y_{1A} - \bar y_{1B}\) the good drivers’ \(-\delta \) cancels, and in \(\bar y_{2A} - \bar y_{2B}\) the rough drivers’ \(+\delta \) cancels, so only the errors remain: \[ \sigma _2^2 = \frac 14\cdot 4\cdot \frac {\sigma ^2}{n/2} = \frac {2\sigma ^2}{n} . \] □

The driver effect has not been reduced by strategy (ii); it has been removed from the comparison entirely, by balancing it across the brands. Strategy (c) goes further. Each \(d_k = \mu _A - \mu _B + \varepsilon _{kA} - \varepsilon _{kB}\) is free of every car effect, known or unknown, so with \(n\) cars \[ \Var (\bar d) = \frac {2\sigma ^2}{n} . \] In strategy (a), on the other hand, the car-to-car variation \(\sigma _\beta ^2\) enters each observation, and \(\Var (\bar y_A - \bar y_B) = 2(\sigma _\beta ^2 + \sigma ^2)/n\) with \(2n\) cars. Blocking on cars therefore gives the same number of comparisons from half the cars, with the variance reduced by the factor \(\sigma ^2/(\sigma _\beta ^2 + \sigma ^2)\). When cars differ a great deal, which in a wear study they do, the gain is large. Strategy (c) is the paired comparison design of the next section, the simplest case of the randomised block design.
