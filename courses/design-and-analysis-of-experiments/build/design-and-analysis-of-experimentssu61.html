10.6 Practice problems

Problem 10.6.1. Explain how to generate the one-half fraction of the \(2^k\) design of the highest resolution, and give it for \(k = 4\).

Show solution

Solution. Use the defining relation \(I = ABC\cdots K\), with all \(k\) letters, which gives resolution \(k\). Write down the full \(2^{k-1}\) design in the first \(k - 1\) factors and set the level of the last factor in each run equal to the product of the levels of the others. For \(k = 4\), \(D = ABC\): the runs are \((1)\), \(ad\), \(bd\), \(ab\), \(cd\), \(ac\), \(bc\), \(abcd\), the \(2^{4-1}_{\mathrm {IV}}\) design with \(I = ABCD\).

Problem 10.6.2. A \(2^4\) experiment is to be run as a quarter fraction using \(ABC\) and \(BCD\) as defining contrasts.

(a)
Obtain the principal quarter fraction and its alias structure.
(b)
The full \(2^4\) was in fact run, with \((1) = 19\), \(a = 15\), \(b = 15\), \(ab = 18\), \(c = 15\), \(ac = 18\), \(bc = 17\), \(abc = 19\), \(d = 13\), \(ad = 18\), \(bd = 18\), \(abd = 18\), \(cd = 18\), \(acd = 19\), \(bcd = 18\) and \(abcd = 20\). Using only the runs of your quarter fraction, estimate the main effects \(A\), \(B\) and \(D\), and comment.

Show solution

Solution. (a) The runs with an even number of letters from both \(\{a, b, c\}\) and \(\{b, c, d\}\) are \((1)\), \(bc\), \(abd\), \(acd\), the principal block of the confounding scheme in Section 9. On them \(ABC = BCD = -\), so the defining relation is \(I = -ABC = -BCD = AD\), including the generalised interaction \(ABC \cdot BCD = AD\). The aliases are \begin {gather*} A = D = -BC = -ABCD , \\ B = ABD = -AC = -CD , \\ C = ACD = -AB = -BD . \end {gather*} The word \(AD\) has only two letters: the design has resolution II, and the main effects \(A\) and \(D\) are aliased with each other.

(b) The four responses are \((1) = 19\), \(bc = 17\), \(abd = 18\), \(acd = 19\). Each estimate is a contrast divided by \(2\): \begin {gather*} \hat A = \tfrac 12(18 + 19 - 19 - 17) = 0.5 , \\ \hat B = \tfrac 12(17 + 18 - 19 - 19) = -1.5 , \\ \hat D = \tfrac 12(18 + 19 - 19 - 17) = 0.5 . \end {gather*} The estimates of \(A\) and \(D\) are identical, because the \(A\) and \(D\) columns coincide on these runs: the design cannot distinguish the two factors at all. Choosing two three-factor interactions whose product is a two-factor interaction is sound for blocking, where the confounded effects are lost to blocks, but disastrous for fractionation, where the generalised interaction becomes a word of the defining relation. For a quarter fraction of a \(2^4\) the best possible is resolution II in any case, which is why such small fractions are rarely used with four factors.

Problem 10.6.3. Consider the \(2^{5-2}\) design with generators \(I = ABCD\) and \(I = CDE\).

(a)
Write down the complete alias structure, and find the resolution.
(b)
Obtain the principal quarter fraction and the remaining three quarter fractions.
(c)
The full \(2^5\) experiment gave the data below. Using the principal quarter fraction, estimate the effects \(A\), \(E\) and \(BD\).

\((1) = 21\) \(d = 33\) \(e = 30\) \(de = 29\)
\(a = 33\) \(ad = 29\) \(ae = 31\) \(ade = 36\)
\(b = 28\) \(bd = 30\) \(be = 33\) \(bde = 25\)
\(ab = 26\) \(abd = 27\) \(abe = 24\) \(abde = 29\)
\(c = 34\) \(cd = 22\) \(ce = 26\) \(cde = 31\)
\(ac = 34\) \(acd = 36\) \(ace = 26\) \(acde = 35\)
\(bc = 24\) \(bcd = 27\) \(bce = 35\) \(bcde = 27\)
\(abc = 25\) \(abcd = 36\) \(abce = 30\) \(abcde = 24\)

Show solution

Solution. (a) The generalised interaction is \(ABCD \cdot CDE = ABE\), so \(I = ABCD = CDE = ABE\), of resolution III. The alias sets are

\(A = BCD = ACDE = BE\)
\(B = ACD = BCDE = AE\)
\(C = ABD = DE = ABCE\)
\(D = ABC = CE = ABDE\)
\(E = ABCDE = CD = AB\)
\(AC = BD = ADE = BCE\)
\(AD = BC = ACE = BDE\)

(b) The four quarter fractions are given by the signs of \(ABCD\) and \(CDE\):

fraction runs
\(ABCD = +\), \(CDE = +\) \(ac\), \(bc\), \(ad\), \(bd\), \(e\), \(abe\), \(cde\), \(abcde\)
\(ABCD = +\), \(CDE = -\) \((1)\), \(ab\), \(cd\), \(abcd\), \(ace\), \(bce\), \(ade\), \(bde\)
\(ABCD = -\), \(CDE = +\) \(c\), \(abc\), \(d\), \(abd\), \(ae\), \(be\), \(acde\), \(bcde\)
\(ABCD = -\), \(CDE = -\) \(a\), \(b\), \(acd\), \(bcd\), \(ce\), \(abce\), \(de\), \(abde\)

The first is the principal fraction. (It does not contain \((1)\), because \(CDE\) has an odd number of letters and is \(-\) at \((1)\); the fraction containing \((1)\) is the second, \(I = ABCD = -CDE = -ABE\).)

(c) In the principal fraction the responses are \(ac = 34\), \(bc = 24\), \(ad = 29\), \(bd = 30\), \(e = 30\), \(abe = 24\), \(cde = 31\), \(abcde = 24\). Each estimate is the contrast divided by \(4\): \begin {align*} \hat A &= \tfrac 14\bigl [(34 + 29 + 24 + 24) - (24 + 30 + 30 + 31)\bigr ] = -4/4 = -1.0 , \\ \hat E &= \tfrac 14\bigl [(30 + 24 + 31 + 24) - (34 + 24 + 29 + 30)\bigr ] = -8/4 = -2.0 , \\ \widehat {BD} &= \tfrac 14\bigl [(34 + 30 + 30 + 31) - (24 + 29 + 24 + 24)\bigr ] \\ &= \tfrac 14(125 - 115) = 10/4 = 2.5 . \end {align*}

By the alias structure these estimate \(A + BE + BCD + ACDE\), \(E + AB + CD + ABCDE\) and \(BD + AC + ADE + BCE\); in particular the estimate of \(BD\) is equally an estimate of \(AC\).

Problem 10.6.4. A \(2^{3-1}_{\mathrm {III}}\) design with \(I = ABC\) gives estimates \(l_A\), \(l_B\), \(l_C\). The alternate fraction \(I = -ABC\) is then run, giving \(l_A'\), \(l_B'\), \(l_C'\). Show how to estimate the main effects free of two-factor interactions, and identify the resulting design.

Show solution

Solution. The first fraction estimates \(A + BC\), the second \(A - BC\) (Theorem 10.1.2). So \(\frac 12(l_A + l_A')\) estimates \(A\) and \(\frac 12(l_A - l_A')\) estimates \(BC\), and similarly for the others. For three factors the alternate fraction is also the full fold-over (reversing all three signs maps \(a\) to \(bc\), and so on), and the two fractions together form the full \(2^3\) design, in which nothing is aliased.
