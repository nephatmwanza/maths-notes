12.4 Practice problems

Problem 12.4.1. Suppose the four machines of Example 12.1.3 are the only machines in the factory, while the operators are still a random sample. What changes in the model, the tests and the conclusions? Estimate the machine effects.

Show solution

Solution. Machines become fixed, with \(\sum \tau _i = 0\), and operators (nested, random) give the middle column of Theorem 12.1.2: \[ E(MS_{\mathrm {Machines}}) = \sigma ^2 + 2\sigma ^2_\beta + \frac {6\sum \tau _i^2}3 . \] The tests are numerically unchanged: machines against operators (\(F_0 = 3.42\), \(P = 0.073\)) and operators against error (\(F_0 = 4.17\)). But the machine hypothesis is now \(\tau _1 = \dots = \tau _4 = 0\), about these four machines, and instead of \(\sigma ^2_\tau \) one estimates the effects \(\hat \tau _i = \bar y_{i\cdot \cdot } - \bar y_{\cdot \cdot \cdot }\): \[ \hat \tau _1 = 68.67 - 65.83 = 2.83 , \quad \hat \tau _2 = 17.33 , \quad \hat \tau _3 = -3.33 , \quad \hat \tau _4 = -16.83 . \]

Problem 12.4.2. In a study of a chemical’s purity, five batches were chosen at random, two samples were taken from each batch, and two determinations were made on each sample. The mean squares were \(24.0\) for batches, \(6.2\) for samples within batches and \(1.4\) for determinations. Write down the model and the expected mean squares, test the two hypotheses, and estimate the variance components.

Show solution

Solution. The design is nested, with samples within batches and determinations within samples. The model is \[ y_{ijk} = \mu + \tau _i + \beta _{j(i)} + \varepsilon _{(ij)k} , \] with batches \(\tau _i \sim N(0, \sigma ^2_\tau )\), samples \(\beta _{j(i)} \sim N(0, \sigma ^2_\beta )\) and determination errors \(\varepsilon \sim N(0, \sigma ^2)\); \(a = 5\), \(b = 2\), \(n = 2\), and the degrees of freedom are \(4\), \(5\) and \(10\). The expected mean squares are \(\sigma ^2 + 2\sigma ^2_\beta + 4\sigma ^2_\tau \), \(\sigma ^2 + 2\sigma ^2_\beta \) and \(\sigma ^2\).

Samples: \(F_0 = 6.2/1.4 = 4.43 > F_{0.05, 5, 10} = 3.33\) (\(P = 0.022\)), significant. Batches: \(F_0 = 24.0/6.2 = 3.87 < F_{0.05, 4, 5} = 5.19\) (\(P = 0.085\)), not significant. The estimates are \[ \hat \sigma ^2 = 1.4 , \qquad \hat \sigma ^2_\beta = \frac {6.2 - 1.4}2 = 2.4 , \qquad \hat \sigma ^2_\tau = \frac {24.0 - 6.2}4 = 4.45 . \] As with the machines, the largest component is not significant, because only five batches were sampled.

Problem 12.4.3. In Example 12.2.3, test whether V2 outyields V1 under full irrigation, and whether full irrigation outyields none for variety V2, each with a \(t\) test at the \(5\%\) level.

Show solution

Solution. V2 against V1 under I3 is a comparison of subplot levels at one whole-plot level: the difference is \(7.475 - 6.375 = 1.10\) with standard error \(0.134\) on \(18\) degrees of freedom, so \(t = 8.2\), far beyond \(t_{0.025, 18} = 2.10\).

I3 against I1 for V2 compares whole-plot levels at one subplot level: the difference is \(7.475 - 5.45 = 2.03\) with standard error \(0.366\). The degrees of freedom are \[ \hat \nu = \frac {\bigl (2(0.0358) + 0.7305\bigr )^2}{\bigl (2(0.0358)\bigr )^2/18 + 0.7305^2/6} = 7.2 , \] and \(t = 5.5 > t_{0.025, 7.2} = 2.35\). Both differences are significant, but the second, three times the size of its standard error rather than eight, is judged against the much larger whole-plot variation.

Problem 12.4.4. Reanalyse Example 12.3.1 with spacing fixed.

Show solution

Solution. With location random and inoculation and spacing fixed, the restricted rule gives \begin {align*} E(MS_L) &= \sigma ^2 + 8\sigma ^2_L , & E(MS_S) &= \sigma ^2 + 2\sigma ^2_{LS} + 6Q_S , \\ E(MS_\tau ) &= \sigma ^2 + 4\sigma ^2_{L\tau } + 12Q_\tau , & E(MS_{LS}) &= \sigma ^2 + 2\sigma ^2_{LS} , \\ E(MS_{L\tau }) &= \sigma ^2 + 4\sigma ^2_{L\tau } , & E(MS_{\tau S}) &= \sigma ^2 + 3Q_{\tau S} . \end {align*}

Inoculation now has an exact test against its whole-plot error: \(F_0 = 96.0/2.375 = 40.4 > F_{0.05, 1, 2} = 18.51\) (\(P = 0.024\)). Spacing is still tested against location \(\times \) spacing (\(F_0 = 13.51\)), and inoculation \(\times \) spacing against the residual (\(F_0 = 17.78\)). Location is tested against the residual: \(F_0 = 1.625/0.375 = 4.33 < F_{0.05, 2, 6} = 5.14\). The conclusions are the same, but the inoculation test has only \(2\) denominator degrees of freedom, a reminder that three locations give little replication of the whole-plot factor.

Problem 12.4.5. An experiment studies the effect of temperature and heat-treatment time on the strength of steel. Two temperatures and three times are used. The oven is heated to one temperature and three specimens are inserted; one is removed after \(10\) minutes, one after \(20\) and one after \(30\). The temperature is then changed to the other level and the process repeated. The whole experiment was run in four shifts. Shift and temperature are random factors and time is fixed, and the three-factor interaction is assumed negligible.

(a)
What design is this?
(b)
Write down the model and find the expected mean squares.
(c)
The analysis gave \(SS = 240.458\) for shift \(\times \) temperature, \(478.417\) for shift \(\times \) time, \(795.250\) for temperature \(\times \) time, \(244.417\) for error and \(4403.625\) in total. Give the degrees of freedom and test the interactions. How would the main effects be tested?

Show solution

Solution. (a) A split-plot design in blocks. Each shift is a block. Within a shift, the oven run at one temperature is a whole plot, so temperature is the whole-plot factor; the three specimens in a run are the subplots, and time is the subplot factor. Temperature is changed only once per shift.

(b) With \(i\) for shift (\(4\), random), \(j\) for temperature (\(2\), random) and \(k\) for time (\(3\), fixed), \[ y_{ijk} = \mu + S_i + T_j + (ST)_{ij} + \gamma _k + (S\gamma )_{ik} + (T\gamma )_{jk} + \varepsilon _{ijk} , \] with \(S_i\), \(T_j\), \((ST)_{ij}\), \((S\gamma )_{ik}\), \((T\gamma )_{jk}\) normal with mean zero and variances \(\sigma ^2_S, \dots , \sigma ^2_{T\gamma }\) (those involving time restricted to sum to zero over \(k\)), \(\sum _k\gamma _k = 0\), and \(\varepsilon _{ijk} \sim N(0, \sigma ^2)\), all independent. \((ST)_{ij}\) is the whole-plot error. By the restricted rule, with \(Q_\gamma = \sum _k\gamma _k^2/2\), \begin {align*} E(MS_S) &= \sigma ^2 + 3\sigma ^2_{ST} + 6\sigma ^2_S , & E(MS_T) &= \sigma ^2 + 3\sigma ^2_{ST} + 12\sigma ^2_T , \\ E(MS_{ST}) &= \sigma ^2 + 3\sigma ^2_{ST} , & E(MS_\gamma ) &= \sigma ^2 + 2\sigma ^2_{S\gamma } + 4\sigma ^2_{T\gamma } + 8Q_\gamma , \\ E(MS_{S\gamma }) &= \sigma ^2 + 2\sigma ^2_{S\gamma } , & E(MS_{T\gamma }) &= \sigma ^2 + 4\sigma ^2_{T\gamma } , \end {align*}

and \(E(MSE) = \sigma ^2\).

(c) The degrees of freedom are \(3\) (shift), \(1\) (temperature), \(3\) (shift \(\times \) temperature), \(2\) (time), \(6\) (shift \(\times \) time), \(2\) (temperature \(\times \) time) and \(6\) (error), \(23\) in all. With \(MSE = 244.417/6 = 40.74\):

  • shift \(\times \) temperature: \(MS = 80.15\), \(F_0 = 1.97 < F_{0.05, 3, 6} = 4.76\);
  • shift \(\times \) time: \(MS = 79.74\), \(F_0 = 1.96 < F_{0.05, 6, 6} = 4.28\);
  • temperature \(\times \) time: \(MS = 397.63\), \(F_0 = 9.76 > F_{0.05, 2, 6} = 5.14\) (\(P = 0.013\)).

The effect of time depends on the temperature. The remaining \(2645.083\) is the total of the three main-effect sums of squares, which the information given does not separate. Shift and temperature are tested against shift \(\times \) temperature, and time needs a quasi-\(F\) test, \[ F_0 = \frac {MS_\gamma }{MS_{S\gamma } + MS_{T\gamma } - MSE} , \] since no single mean square has expectation \(\sigma ^2 + 2\sigma ^2_{S\gamma } + 4\sigma ^2_{T\gamma }\).
