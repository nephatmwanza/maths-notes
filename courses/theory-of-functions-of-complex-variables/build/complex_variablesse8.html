8 Improper Integrals Using Contour Integration

We intend to evaluate convergent improper integrals of the form \[\int ^{\infty }_{-\infty } f(x)dx\hspace {0.9cm} \text {and}\hspace {0.9cm} \int ^{\infty }_0 g(x)dx\] provided the function \(g(x)\) can be restricted to a suitable complex function \(f(x)\).

This involves integrating a complex function \(f(z)\) around a closed contour \(\Gamma _R\) bounding a domain \(D\) of the type

The integral is evaluated using either the Cauchy integral theorem or the Cauchy Gaursat, letting \(R\longrightarrow \infty ,\hspace {0.2cm} r\longrightarrow 0\) we have the improper integrals.

If fig (a), the contour \(\Gamma _R\) to be traversed is the line \(OA\) along the positive \(x-\)axis, the circular arc \(C_R\) is of radius \(R\) centered at \(z = 0\) ate the vertical line \(BO\).

In fig (b), the contour \(\Gamma _R\) to be traversed is the line \(AB\) along the positive \(x-\)axis the circular arc \(C_R\) from \(B\) to \(C\) centered at \(z = 0\) with radius \(R\), the \(C_r\) from \(D\) to \(A\) with radius \(r\) centered at \(z = a\).

The construction in (b) is used when the function has a singularity at \(z = a\). This is called indenting the contour.

The value of the integral of \(f(z)\) around \(\Gamma _R\) determined either by Cauchy Gaursat or \(C-I\) Cauchy integral is equal to the sum of the segments of \(\Gamma _R\).

For suitable \(f(z)\) the value of the integral along \(C_R\) tends to zero as \(R\longrightarrow \infty \) and if the contour is indented at the origin, the integral along \(C_r\) as \(r\longrightarrow 0\) can be found straight away.
