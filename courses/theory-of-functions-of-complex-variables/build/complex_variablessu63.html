11.2 Analytic Functions, Cauchy–Riemann and the Identity Theorem

Problem 11.10. Prove the following version of L’Hôpital’s rule. Assume \(f\) and \(g\) are analytic near a point \(a \in \mathbb {C}\), and that at \(z = a\) the function \(f\) has a zero of multiplicity \(\ell \) while \(g\) has a zero of multiplicity \(m\), with \(0 \leq \ell , m < \infty \). (A zero of multiplicity \(0\) means the function itself is non-zero at \(z = a\).) Then \[\lim _{z \rightarrow a}\dfrac {f(z)}{g(z)} = \begin {cases} 0 & \ell > m,\\[2pt] \dfrac {f^{(m)}(a)}{g^{(m)}(a)} & \ell = m,\\[6pt] \infty & \ell < m. \end {cases}\]

Problem 11.11. Let \(G\) be a domain and let \(f\) and \(g\) be analytic on \(G\) with \(fg = 0\) on \(G\). Show that either \(f = 0\) or \(g = 0\) on \(G\).

Problem 11.12. Let \(f\) be analytic everywhere in a region \(D\). Prove, using the Cauchy–Riemann equations, that if \(f(z)\) is real-valued for all \(z\) in \(D\) then \(f\) is constant throughout \(D\).

Problem 11.13. Let \(D \subseteq \mathbb {C}\) be open and connected, let \(f\) and \(g\) be holomorphic on \(D\) with \(f(z) \neq 0 \neq g(z)\) for all \(z \in D\), and let \((a_n)_{n \geq 1}\) be a convergent sequence in \(D\) with \(a = \lim _{n\rightarrow \infty } a_n \in D\) and \(a_n \neq a\) for all \(n\). Show that if \[\dfrac {f'(a_n)}{f(a_n)} = \dfrac {g'(a_n)}{g(a_n)} \hspace {0.4cm}\text {for all } n,\] then there is a constant \(c\) with \(f(z) = c\,g(z)\) for all \(z \in D\).

Problem 11.14. Let \(D\) be an open connected region such that \(z \in D\) implies \(\overline {z} \in D\), and let \(f : D \rightarrow \mathbb {C}\) be holomorphic.

(a).
Show that \(g : D \rightarrow \mathbb {C}\) defined by \(g(z) = \overline {f\!\left (\overline {z}\right )}\) is also holomorphic.
(b).
Prove that if \(f(r) \in \mathbb {R}\) for each \(r \in D \cap \mathbb {R}\), then \(f(z) = \overline {f\!\left (\overline {z}\right )}\) for all \(z \in D\).

Problem 11.15. Let \(a\) be a zero of the Riemann zeta function \(\zeta \) in the critical strip \(0 \leq \operatorname {Re}(z) \leq 1\). Prove that \(\overline {a}\), \(1 - a\) and \(1 - \overline {a}\) are also zeros of \(\zeta \).

Problem 11.16. Prove that if a complex-valued function is differentiable then it is analytic.
