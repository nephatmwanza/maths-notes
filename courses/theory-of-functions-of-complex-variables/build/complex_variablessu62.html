9.4 The Argument Principle

Suppose that \(f\) is meromophic on and inside a simple closed curve \(\Gamma \) with zeroes \(a_i\) and poles \(b_k\, ( 1\leq j\leq l_1,\, 1\leq k \leq l_2)\). Suppose none of \(a_j\) or \(b_k\) lie on \(\Gamma \). Then \[\frac {1}{2\pi i}\int _{\Gamma } \frac {f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz = M - N\] where \(M\) is the sum of orders of zeroes at \(a_j\, (1\leq j\leq l_1)\) and \(N\) is the sum of orders of poles at \(b_k \, (1\leq k\leq l_2)\).

Proof. Let \(f(z) = (z - a_k)^{h_k}\,,\, f_1(z) \) then (zero at \(a_k\) has order \(h_k)\) \[\frac {f'(z)}{f(z)} = \frac {h_k}{z - a_k} + \frac {f_1'(z)}{f_1(z)}\]

So \(\frac {f'(z)}{f(z)}\) has a simple pole at \(a_k\) and \(\operatorname {Res}\Big [\frac {f'(z)}{f(z)}, a_k\Big ] = h_k\quad (1\leq k \leq l_1)\)

Let \(f(z) = (z - b_j)^{-m_j} f_2(z)\) where \(f\) has a pole of order \(m_j\) at \(b_j\, (1\leq j \leq l_2)\). \[\frac {f'(z)}{f(z)} = \frac {z^{-m_j}}{z - b_j} + \frac {f'_2(z)}{f_2(z)}\]

\[\operatorname {Res}\Big [\frac {f'(z)}{f(z)}, b_j\Big ] = -m_j\] By residue theorem \begin {align*} \int _{\Gamma } \frac {f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz & = 2\pi i \sum ^{l_1}_{k = 1}\operatorname {Res}\Big [\frac {f'(z)}{f(z)}, a_k\Big ] + 2\pi i \sum ^{l_2}_{j = 1}\operatorname {Res}\Big [\frac {f'(z)}{f(z)}, b_j\Big ]\\\\ \int _{\Gamma } \frac {f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz & = 2\pi i (M - N)\\\\ \end {align*} □

Application of Cauchy Residue Theorem to Evaluate of Definite Integrals

Example 9.23.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle {\int ^{\infty }_0 \frac {dx}{1 + x^{2n}}}\)

\(\frac {1}{1 + z^{2n}}\) has simple poles at \(2n^{\text {th}}\) roots of \(-1\)

By Cauchy residue theorem \[ \int _{\Gamma } \frac {1}{1 + z^{2n}}dz = 2\pi i \operatorname {Res} \Big \{ \frac {1}{1 + z^{2n}}, e^{i\pi /2n}\Big \}\]

\begin {align*} \int ^R_0\frac {1}{1 + x^{2n}}\, dx + \int _{C_R}\frac {1}{1 + z^{2n}}\, dz + \int _{\gamma }\frac {1}{1 + z^{2n}}\, dz & = 2\pi i \Bigg [\lim _{z \rightarrow e^{i\pi /2n}}\Big (z - e^{i\pi /2n}\Big )\frac {1}{1 + z^{2n}}\Bigg ]\\\\ & = \frac {-e^{i\pi /2n}}{2n} \end {align*}

On \(C_R\)

\(\displaystyle {\left |\displaystyle {\int _{C_R} \frac {1}{1 + z^{2n}}dz}\right |\leq \frac {\pi R}{nR^{2n - 1}}\longrightarrow 0 }\) as \(R\longrightarrow \infty \)


On \(\gamma \quad z = re^{i\pi / n}\quad (0\leq r\leq R)\)

\begin {align*} \int _{\gamma } \frac {dz}{1 + z^{2n}} & = \int ^0_R\frac {e^{i\pi /n}\,dr}{1 + \Big (re^{i\pi /n}\Big )^{2n}}\\\\ & = -e^{i\pi /n}\int ^R_0\frac {dr}{4r^{2n}}\\\\ & = -e^{i\pi /n}\int _0^{\infty } \frac {dx}{1 + x^{2n}}\quad (\text {letting}\quad R\longrightarrow \infty ) \end {align*}

\[L.H.S = \Big (1 - e^{i\pi /n}\Big ) \int ^{\infty }_0 \frac {dx}{1 + x^{2n}} = -\frac {i\pi e^{i\pi /2n}}{n}\]

\[\implies \quad \int ^{\infty }_0 \frac {dx}{1 + x^{2n}} = \frac {\pi }{2n\sin \Big (\frac {\pi }{2n}\Big )}\]
