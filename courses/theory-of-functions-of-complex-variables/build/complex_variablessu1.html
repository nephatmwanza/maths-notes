1.1 The Complex Plane

It is natural to associate the complex number \(z = x + iy\) with a point in the plane whose Cartesian coordinates are \(x\) and \(y\). The number \(z\) can be thought of as a directed line segment or a vector from the origin, to the point \((x,y)\).

Definition
Let \(z = x + iy\) be any complex number then the modulus of \(z\) denoted \(\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}\) is the non-negative real number \(\hspace {0.2cm} \begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix} = \sqrt {x^2 + y^2}\)

Remark
While the statement \(\hspace {0.1cm} z_1 < z_2\hspace {0.1cm}\) is in general meaningless, \(\hspace {0.1cm}\begin {vmatrix} z_1\\ \end {vmatrix} < \begin {vmatrix} z_2\\ \end {vmatrix}\hspace {0.1cm}\) means that the points corresponding to \(z_1\) is closer to the origin than the point corresponding to \(z_2\).

Definition
The distance between the points representing the complex numbers \(z_1\) and \(z_2\) is given by \[\begin {vmatrix} z_1 - z_2\\ \end {vmatrix} = \sqrt {(x_1 -x_2)^2 + (y_1- y_2)^2}\]

It is important to note that

1.
\(\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}\) distance in the complex plane of a complex number from \(0\).
2.
\(\begin {vmatrix} z_1 - z_2\\ \end {vmatrix}\) distance in the plane from \(z_1\) to \(z_2\)
3.
\(z\cdot \overline {z} = \begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2\hspace {0.3cm}\) i.e \(\hspace {0.3cm} \begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 = \big (Re z\big )^2 + \big (Im z\big )^2\)
4.
\(Re (z) \leq \begin {vmatrix} Re(z)\\ \end {vmatrix} \leq \begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}\hspace {0.2cm}\) and \(\hspace {0.2cm} Im (z) \leq \begin {vmatrix} Im (z)\\ \end {vmatrix} \leq \begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}\)
5.
\(\begin {vmatrix} z_1 + z_2\\ \end {vmatrix} \leq \begin {vmatrix} z_1\\ \end {vmatrix} + \begin {vmatrix} z_2\\ \end {vmatrix}\hspace {0.2cm}\) triangle inequality and the equality holds if and only if \(z_1\) and \(z_2\) lie on the same (half ray) through the origin in the complex plane.

Example
Describe the set of points \(z\) in the complex plane that satisfy \(\hspace {0.2cm} \begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix} = \begin {vmatrix} z - i\\ \end {vmatrix}\)

Solution
Let \(\hspace {0.2cm} z = x + iy\hspace {0.2cm}\) then \begin {align*} \begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix} & = \begin {vmatrix} z - i\\ \end {vmatrix}\\\\ \implies \hspace {0.5cm}\begin {vmatrix} x + iy\\ \end {vmatrix} & = \begin {vmatrix} x + iy - i\\ \end {vmatrix}\\ \implies \hspace {0.5cm} \sqrt {x^2 + y^2} & = \sqrt {x^2 + (y - 1)^2 }\\ x^2 + y^2 & = x^2 + (y - 1)^2\\ y^2 & = y^2 - 2y + 1\\\\ \implies \hspace {0.5cm} y = \dfrac {1}{2} \end {align*}

Since the equality is true for arbitrary \(x\), \(\hspace {0.2cm} y = \dfrac {1}{2}\) is an equation on the horizontal line. This the complex numbers satisfying \(\hspace {0.2cm}\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix} = \begin {vmatrix} z - i\\ \end {vmatrix}\hspace {0.2cm}\) can be written as \(\hspace {0.2cm} z = x + \dfrac {1}{2}i\)
