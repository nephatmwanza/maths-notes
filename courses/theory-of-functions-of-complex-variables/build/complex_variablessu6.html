1.6 Lines

Suppose we know that the complex point lies on line, and it makes an angle \(\theta \) with the real axis, so its slope is \(\tan \theta \). Then \[arg(z - a) = \begin {cases} \theta \\ \pi - \theta \\ \end {cases}\] defines the locus of points \(z\) on the line.

Also, \(arg(z - a) = \theta \) represents a half line from a fixed point \(a\).

Suppose the required line is the perpendicular bisector of the line joining two points \(a\) and \(b\). Then the locus of \(z\) is given by \(\hspace {0.2cm} \begin {vmatrix} z - a\\ \end {vmatrix} = \begin {vmatrix} z - b\\ \end {vmatrix}\)

Example
Trace the locus defined by \(\hspace {0.2cm} arg(z = 3) = \tan ^{-1}(2)\)

Solution
Let \(z = x + iy\), then \(\hspace {0.2cm} arg(x + 3 + iy) = \tan ^{-1}(2)\)

\[\tan ^{-1}\Bigg (\dfrac {y}{x + 3}\Bigg ) = \tan ^{-1}(2)\]

\[\dfrac {y}{x + 3} = 2\]

\[\implies \hspace {0.3cm} y = 2x + 6\]

\(\mathbb {C}\cup \{\infty \}\)
Consider \(\mathbb {C}\) as contained in \(\mathbb {R}^3\) in the form \(\hspace {0.1cm} x + iy \longleftrightarrow (x,y) \longleftrightarrow (x,y,0)\) in the \(x-y\) plane in \(\mathbb {R}^3\).

Consider \(\hspace {0.2cm} S = \big \{ (x,y,z):\hspace {0.1cm} x^2 + y^2 +z^2 = 1\big \}\)

When \(z = 0:\hspace {0.2cm} x^2 + y^2 = 1\)

\(\mathcal {Z} = t(0,0,1) + (1-t)(x,y,0)\), \(\hspace {0.2cm} t \in \mathbb {R}, \hspace {0.2cm} z \in \mathbb {C}\) is a straight line in \(\mathbb {R}^3\) connecting \((0,0,1)\) to \((x,y,z), \hspace {0.2cm} z \in \mathbb {C}\).

\(\mathcal {Z} = t\big ((1-t)x, (1-t)y, t\big )\)

Putting on a unit sphere, we have \[\big (1 - t\big )^2 x^2 + \big (1 - t\big )^2 y^2 + t^2 = 1\] Since \(\hspace {0.2cm} \begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 = x^2 + y^2\) \(\implies \hspace {0.2cm} \big (1 -t\big )^2\hspace {0.1cm}\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 + t^2 = 1\)

\(t\neq 1\hspace {0.1cm}\) this corresponds to \((0,0,1)\)

\begin {align*} \implies \hspace {0.5cm} 1 - t^2 & = \big (1- t\big )^2\hspace {0.1cm}\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2\\\\ 1 - t^2 & = \begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 + t^2 \begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 - 2t\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 \end {align*}

\[\implies \hspace {0.3cm} t^2\big (\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 + 1\big ) - 2 t \begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 + \begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 - 1= 0\]

\[ t = \dfrac {\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 - 1}{\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 + 1}\]

\begin {align*} \therefore \hspace {0.3cm} \mathcal {Z} & = \Bigg ( \dfrac {2x}{\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 + 1}\hspace {0.2cm},\hspace {0.2cm} \dfrac {2y}{\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 + 1}\hspace {0.2cm},\hspace {0.2cm} \dfrac {\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 - 1}{\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 + 1}\Bigg )\\\\ & = \Bigg ( \dfrac {z + \overline {z}}{\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 + 1}\hspace {0.2cm},\hspace {0.2cm} \dfrac {-\big (z - \overline {z}\big )}{\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 + 1}\hspace {0.2cm},\hspace {0.2cm} \dfrac {\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 - 1}{\begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2 + 1}\Bigg ) \end {align*}

If we are given a point \(\mathcal {Z}\) on \(S\), then \(z\) on \(\mathbb {C}\) can be found \(\hspace {0.1cm} z = \dfrac {x_1 + ix_2}{1 - x_3}\hspace {0.1cm}\) where \(\mathcal {Z} = (x_1, x_2, x_3)\)

\(\mathbb {C}\cup \{\infty \} \longleftrightarrow S\longleftarrow \) Riemann sphere

Can give a distance function on \(\hspace {0.1cm} \mathbb {C}\cup \{\infty \}\)

\[d(z,z') = \begin {cases} d(\mathcal {Z},\mathcal {Z}')\hspace {0.2cm} (\text {in}\hspace {0.1cm}\mathbb {R}^3)\hspace {0.2cm}\text {where}\hspace {0.2cm}z,z' \neq \infty \\\\ \dfrac {2}{\Big (1 + \begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2\Big )^{1/2}}\hspace {0.4cm}\text {if}\hspace {0.4cm}z' = \infty \\ \end {cases} \]

\[d(z,z') = \begin {cases} \Big [\big (x_1 -x_1'\big )^2 + \big (x_2 - x'_2\big )^2 + \big (x_3 - x'_3\big )\Big ]^{1/2}\\\\ \dfrac {2}{\Big (1 + \begin {vmatrix} z\\ \end {vmatrix}^2\Big )^{1/2}}\hspace {0.4cm}\text {if}\hspace {0.4cm}z' = \infty \\ \end {cases} \]

This correspondence is called sterographic projection

\begin {align*} \mathbb {C}\cup \{\infty \}\hspace {2cm} a + \infty & = \infty \hspace {0.5cm} a\in \mathbb {C}\\ a\cdot \infty & = \infty \hspace {0.5cm} a\in \mathbb {C}\hspace {0.2cm},\hspace {0.5cm} a\neq 0\\ \dfrac {a}{\infty } & = 0\hspace {0.5cm} a\in \mathbb {C} \end {align*}
