16.6 Review of the course

The course has followed one idea, optimisation under constraints, from its linear core outwards.

Linear programming. The feasible region is a polyhedron and the optimum is at a vertex (the sections on models and geometry); the simplex method walks between vertices, in tableau or revised form (the next two sections); duality supplies certificates of optimality, shadow prices, and sensitivity ranges (the sections on duality and sensitivity).

Structure. Transportation, assignment and network problems have totally unimodular matrices, so their LPs give integer answers and special algorithms are fast (the sections on transportation and networks). General integer programmes need branch and bound and cutting planes; non-linear ones the KKT conditions, which extend duality; and staged problems dynamic programming (the next three sections).

Uncertainty. Decisions under risk use expected values and the value of information; games use minimax, which is LP duality again; inventories, queues and Markov models give formulas for the effect of randomness; and simulation handles what the formulas cannot (the last five sections).

Problem Main tool
allocate scarce resources linear programming, duality
ship, assign, route, schedule a project network flows, total unimodularity
yes–no decisions, fixed costs integer programming
curved costs, risk KKT conditions, quadratic programming
decisions in stages dynamic programming
uncertain states, opponents decision trees, minimax
stock, waiting lines, deterioration EOQ, newsvendor, queues, MDPs
anything else simulation
