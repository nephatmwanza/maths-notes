# When to caption a table or figure

Settled 2026-08-01. Both courses follow this.

## Every figure gets a caption

A diagram is meaningless without one. A reader scanning the page sees the picture before
the surrounding prose, and on a phone the sentence that introduced it may be a screen away.
The caption should say **what the figure shows**, not merely name it — "Positive skew: the
long tail runs to the right and the mean is pulled above the mode" rather than "Skewness".

## Tables: caption only if the table is a *thing*, not the *working*

Ask what happens if the surrounding sentences are removed.

**Caption it** when the table is data the reader may return to or refer back to:
- the data a question is posed on
- a contingency or joint-distribution table
- a summary a later section uses

**Do not caption it** when the table *is* the calculation — an `fX` column being built
inside a solution, a cumulative-frequency row, an ANOVA table that the sentence before it
already announces. Captioning those adds a numbered label to every step of the arithmetic
and makes the page harder to read, not easier.

The test: *if removing the surrounding text would leave the table meaningless, caption it;
if the table is the working, leave it alone.*

## Why numbering still matters on a website

There is no page to flip back to, so cross-references matter less than in print. But the
number is what lets a student ask a question — "I don't follow Figure 2.3" — in the
question box at the foot of the page. That alone justifies it.
